## Phase 1 — Full CRM Replacement

**What was tried:** Built a full gym CRM — billing, scheduling, member management, the whole operational stack, positioned as a replacement for PushPress / Mindbody / Kicksite.

**Real blocker — migration friction.** Existing CRMs hold credit card data hostage. Even when a gym owner was ready to buy (Global MMA's fan-founder was close; one verbal yes at price from another gym), the deal died on the switch cost. Credit card migration is slow or impossible; owners can't move their billing infrastructure on a whim. One verbal yes never turned into a signed deal. Global MMA's fan-founder was enthusiastic but blocked by a cost-wary partner; the deal died there.

**What was learned:** Don't compete on the operational critical path. Migration friction is structural — it isn't solved by a better product or a lower price. The CRM incumbents have a lock that a new entrant cannot break through head-to-head.

## Phase 2 — Retention Layer (two pillars, no customization, no AI angle)

**What changed:** Stripped to a member-facing app sitting *alongside* the gym's existing CRM — engagement loop, points, ranks, content library. No billing, no check-in, no signup. Pitched as a retention add-on.

**The structural diagnosis that shaped this phase.** A full office-hours session (April 26) mapped the layered architecture ceiling: a product that runs alongside a CRM cannot mandate adoption from coaches, owners, or members. Coaches log into the CRM because that's how they get paid. Members book because they have to. A layered product depends entirely on voluntary behavior. This means:
- **Coach-driven personalization (per-class voice notes, per-class summaries)** was evaluated in depth and killed. Per-class coach input requires coaches to open the app daily by choice, without an adoption hook. The only proven coach adoption hooks (pay, schedule, attendance) are owned by the CRM, not a layered app. Every workaround evaluated had the same failure mode.
- **Messaging was briefly added then immediately cut the same day.** After removing coach feedback, member-coach messaging was added to fill the gap. On reflection, it was thinness anxiety masquerading as a product decision — it doesn't move retention the way the loyalty engine does and adds scope without confirmed demand. Cut the same day it was added.
- **The remaining product (loyalty + algorithmic content) was honest but thin.** One real differentiator vs. a gym's existing CRM bundled app: the loyalty engine. Content recommendations are there but generic without per-class coach input. The pitch cleared the intellectual bar but not the wallet bar.

**Real blocker — unit economics.** $50–100/mo ACV with solo founder sales to SMB gym owners. US fighting-gym TAM is ~5–10k businesses. Even fully penetrated, that's a small number. 10 hours of founder time per $100 of MRR added. The math was structurally bad independent of whether the product could sell.

**Secondary blocker — the product looked like a feature.** Without the design/brand angle, the pitch was: "pay us monthly for a loyalty program your CRM doesn't have." Smart gym owners heard it as an add-on, not a product. No visual hook, no obvious "whoa."

## Phase 3 — Retention + AI Brand Customization (current, selling again)

**What changed (May 1, 2026 merge pivot).** A second project (AIAutoDesign — AI-driven brand customization at SaaS pricing) was being evaluated simultaneously. Rather than switching projects entirely, the two were merged: AIAutoDesign's architecture (tokenized design system, AI asset generation pipeline, cross-modal orchestration) becomes the brand-customization surface that CombatDen was missing. The product becomes three surfaces: loyalty, content, brand.

**Why this fixed the unit economics problem.** The $300 and $500 tiers are viable on their own. The freelancer-replacement ROI argument ($15–50k custom app for $300/mo SaaS) gives the higher tiers a standalone justification beyond the retention math. The $100 tier exists as a customer-acquisition instrument below the CRM price reflex (~$200/mo). TAM also expanded from fighting gyms (~5–10k US) to all class-based gyms (~100–200k US), with fighting gyms as the beachhead (founder's warm pipeline and cultural fluency) rather than the market ceiling.

**ICP refinement (May 24, 2026).** Further tightened to a psychographic, not a sport: premium, branding-conscious, retention-focused class-based gym owners with budget. The $300+ price point self-selects for this. Geographic + psychographic concentration (own the premium class-based gyms in one metro starting from the fighting network) rather than sub-vertical-sequential expansion.

**Pipeline simplification (May 20, 2026).** The orchestration architecture shipped as a simple dependency DAG (inputs: two YAMLs; outputs: images, colors, fonts, text) rather than the elaborate six-pillar cascade originally designed. The simpler architecture works, is faster to reason about, and is app-agnostic. Animation and icon modification are on the roadmap post-validation, not dropped.

**Pricing restructure (May 24, 2026).** Moved off depth-of-customization as the pricing axis. New ladder: $100 (shared shell, locked gym-type theme), $300 (your own App Store app, done-for-you redesign at MVP, self-serve agent as destination), $500 flat (multi-location, up to 3), Enterprise (quote). The $1000 fully-custom concierge tier retired — the DAG simplification made custom-grade output cheap enough to deliver at $300.

**Where things stand now (May 30, 2026).** Prototype of the theming pipeline and YouTube-agentic content layer is done and feels genuinely strong. Pre-first-sale. Selling again with this framing.

**Architecture-fit refinement (June 1, 2026).** Working through gym-type market sizing surfaced a structural constraint that reshapes the near-term market. The engagement loop is anchored on *booking* — the booking animation is a peak moment and "book next class" is the CTA that drives the whole retention cycle (`CombatDen:UX_Logic/Core_user_journey.md`) — and booking only works without the CRM in the **unlimited-membership** model, where there's no entitlement gate and no hard class capacity. Fighting gyms were "simple" precisely because of this. In pack/credit/capacity gyms (yoga, pilates, boutique cardio, ClassPass-heavy studios) booking is a scarce transaction requiring real-time entitlement and capacity, knowable only by owning or integrating the CRM. So the active near-term market narrows to unlimited-membership gyms (fighting beachhead now, CrossFit the nearest adjacent), and broadening to other gym types is gated on the operational/CRM layer — which is the deliberate future thesis, not a near-term move. See `CombatDen:Business/pivots/2026-06-01-14-beachhead-unlimited-membership-crm-as-future-layer.md`.

**The test.** If this pivot produces a sale, there's a lot to build. If it doesn't, the project needs a fundamental rethink or a full idea change.

## Phase 4 — Back to the Full CRM

**What changed (June 2, 2026).** A red-team of the finished landing page — the founder role-played a skeptical gym owner advised by a separate AI, feeding it the live site copy and the real sales answers (`CombatDen:ConvoWithClaude.md`) — exposed that the layered product's two deepest flaws (blindness to payment/entitlement/capacity, and the double check-in it forces) make it *added operational burden* pitched at gyms that already hate their CRM. Working through it surfaced the decisive evidence: across the whole arc, the **full CRM is the only thing that ever produced a real buying signal** (a gym at the door, "I'll sign once you bring it back" — died on migration friction, not demand), while the **layer only ever drew "that's cool"** and never advanced a deal. The reasoned pivots away from the CRM (Phases 2–3) walked the project away from its single validated signal.

**Why the CRM is viable now when it wasn't in Phase 1.** Phase 1's CRM died because too few gyms allowed migration. The product is now vertical-agnostic, so the TAM is roughly an order of magnitude larger and the **switch-friendly slice is finally big enough to be a market.** The near-term ICP narrows precisely to **gyms on switch-friendly CRMs** (data-portable, no gigantic export fee, no data hostage) — engineering around migration friction by segment selection rather than abandoning the CRM. "We don't hold your data hostage" becomes anti-lock-in positioning. The ~75%-built Phase 1 billing stack is revivable (caveat: the last 25% — failed payments, proration, refunds, edge cases — is the hard, trust-critical part).

**The discipline.** Validate before building: pitch the whole product to switch-friendly, CRM-hating Dallas gyms; a real yes is a commitment to switch, not "cool." If no commitment, build nothing and walk. See `CombatDen:Business/pivots/2026-06-02-19-return-to-full-crm-switch-friendly-beachhead.md`.

**The test.** Can the founder get a switch-friendly gym to commit to actually switching — the thing "cool" never produced and migration friction killed last time?

**Still open as of July 25, 2026.** The June 2 assignment — define the validation bar, build the switch-friendly-CRM list, rewrite the landing page, then pitch and measure commitment-to-switch — has not been executed. Seven and a half weeks of building happened instead (rewards productionization, member app, kiosk). That gap is the single most important unresolved item in the whole arc.

## Phase 5 — The Autonomous Agent Layer (WITHDRAWN)

> [!warning] Withdrawn two days after it was written
> On **July 27, 2026** the founder examined this thesis more deeply and concluded it will not work: *"i had it yesterady but ive realzied it a very thin idea"* — *"ignore the agentic layer parts it was an idea that now that i dug deeper into it wont work."* The autonomous agent layer is out of scope. `CombatDen:Business/pivots/2026-07-25-16-autonomous-agent-layer-added-to-crm.md` is superseded by [[pivots/2026-07-27-11-customization-engine-is-the-company|pivots/2026-07-27-11-customization-engine-is-the-company.md]]. The phase is kept below as written, unaltered, because a thesis that lasted two days is part of the record.

**What changed (July 25, 2026).** An **autonomous AI agent layer** is added on top of the CRM and becomes the product's primary differentiator and the thesis of the YC F26 application. This is explicitly an *addition, not a reversal* — the founder's words: "First I think i wanna not pivot but actually add onto what i have." Phase 4 stands unchanged underneath it. The trigger was Ploy (ploy.ai), a YC-backed autonomous marketing agent company.

**The mechanism.** Two steps, only the second of which differentiates. **Step 1** is a conversational agent that takes commands and acts in the CRM — parity, not advantage. **Step 2** is the layer that acts *first*: reviews the industry weekly, reviews customers daily, watches competitors' marketing, generates seasonal ideas, all unprompted. The founder's framing: "this is a layer above an ai agent that works after you talk, it's actually autonomous."

**The competitive finding that justifies it.** A verification sweep of live sites on July 25 found PushPress, Mindbody, Momence, Wodify, Zen Planner, Glofox, HealthdeskAI, and 1club all shipping AI — and **every one of them reactive.** You ask, it answers or acts; or it scores churn risk and nudges a human. None runs on its own schedule, does cross-industry comparison, or generates ideas unprompted. PushPress has already shipped Step 1 into the exact beachhead (and markets it as "a general manager that never clocks out," which it is not), so building Step 1 buys parity. Step 2 is unclaimed across the entire verified field. Positioning in one line: **every gym CRM now has an AI you talk to; this is the one you don't have to.**

**Why it can be sold safely.** Nothing executes without approval. The agent is read-only by default; approval granularity splits by surface — **autonomous plans are approved once as standing plans** with scope and limits and then run unattended, while **chatbot actions are approved per action** because the owner is already in the loop when they ask. A safety classifier is a later addition, not MVP.

**What the value argument actually is.** Consistency, not intelligence. The analysis is not hard, but owners do not do it reliably — the job currently falls to a front-desk worker, a young hire, or a friendly coach, done only when there is time. Because that labor is already bundled at no marginal cost, the "replaces a hire you can't afford" ROI argument is dead and must not be used. Owning the full stack is what makes the layer possible at all: most gym CRMs are genuinely closed (Mindbody excepted), so no outside agent can reach the data.

**The window.** Not table stakes today — this is an old, slow-moving industry — but the AI-axis barrier is product conviction, not engineering, and PushPress repositioned its whole platform around AI quickly. Months, not years. See `CombatDen:Business/pivots/2026-07-25-16-autonomous-agent-layer-added-to-crm.md` and `CombatDen:Business/Office_Hours/2026-07-25-16-Office_Hours_Autonomous_Agent_Layer.md`.

**Timeline correction made in the same session.** The project started around November 2025 — roughly **8 months**, much of it idea phase — not the 15 months an earlier draft claimed. Still pre-first-sale.

**The test.** Two, and the second is the older one. (1) Does the plan-authoring step actually get done — does an owner approve a standing plan, or does the agent sit idle waiting for one? (2) The unexecuted June 2 test still stands: will a switch-friendly gym commit to switching?

## Phase 6 — Recompose: the customization engine is the company (current)

**What changed (July 27, 2026).** The AI customization engine — built across Phases 3–5 as an internal capability that made the gym product's design tier possible — becomes the company. CombatDen (the class-based-gym CRM, member app, kiosk, rewards, agentic video feed) is retained as the **reference implementation, live demo canvas, and eval bed**; it is no longer the product being sold and no longer the sales priority. The one-line description written the same night for the YC F26 application: **"Customers agentically redesign white-label apps."** See [[pivots/2026-07-27-11-customization-engine-is-the-company|pivots/2026-07-27-11-customization-engine-is-the-company.md]] and [[Office_Hours/2026-07-27-11-Office_Hours_Customization_Engine_As_The_Company|Office_Hours/2026-07-27-11-Office_Hours_Customization_Engine_As_The_Company.md]].

**The buyer changes, and this is the largest single shift in the whole arc.** From a gym owner to a **multi-tenant white-label SaaS platform with real tenant volume**. Explicitly not agencies, not freelancers, not the end business — at low tenant counts a buyer's bottleneck is finding clients, not producing themes, so volume is the unit of value. The value argument has two halves and neither is tenant satisfaction: **tier-unlock** — deep per-tenant branding is a premium tier platforms either cannot staff or staff expensively, because it costs a designer per tenant ([[Concepts/Business/Pricing/Tier_Unlock_ROI|Tier_Unlock_ROI]]) — and **lost deals**, competitive deals lost to whoever's app can actually look like the prospect's brand. The sharpest wedge is platforms that *already* run a design service (Mighty Pro's Design Lab, Passion.io's DFY tier), because they can read the value off a P&L instead of imagining it. The April 2026 research finding that end users do not pay for brand depth becomes irrelevant under this framing rather than damaging.

**The scope is a full visual redesign, not theming** ([[Concepts/Product/Mechanisms/Full_Visual_Redesign|Full_Visual_Redesign]]). Every screen, every asset, every surface reading as that customer's brand — driven by the tenant, with **no new features**. Because the product now changes structure and not only assets, the labor removed is the designer *and* the front-end engineer who implements the design: the whole custom-build line item ($10–50k, 4–12 weeks in the agency market). The [[Concepts/Business/Strategy/Freelance_Conversion_Thesis|Freelance_Conversion_Thesis]] returns inverted — agencies are not the buyer, but agency-grade work is what the product replaces. The bar is **close enough at a fraction of the price**, explicitly not parity with human designers.

**The mechanism is explicitly two parts.** *Assets are data, so a pipeline generates them:* the existing app-agnostic dependency DAG ([[Concepts/Product/Mechanisms/Orchestrator|Orchestrator]], [[Concepts/Product/Mechanisms/Tokenized_Design_System|Tokenized_Design_System]]) resolves palette, typography, copy, icons and images, with deterministic colour derivation, WCAG AA enforced as an accept condition, bounded and known cost per run, and [[Concepts/Product/Mechanisms/Modal_Coherence|coherence]] achieved structurally rather than by critique. *Layout is code, so an agent writes it:* layout is the surface no slot inventory can express, so an agent edits the view layer directly in the platform's codebase. Deterministic where determinism is available, agentic only where it is required. The split is load-bearing for positioning as well as engineering — one agent doing everything reads as a wrapper over a model; two systems with a clear boundary reads as architecture, and it is what stops the company being filed under "another coding agent."

**Functional equivalence is the safety invariant** ([[Concepts/Product/Mechanisms/No_New_Requests_Invariant|No_New_Requests_Invariant]]). A redesign changes **arrangement only**: no screen merged or split, no functionality added, none removed. Every screen does exactly what it did before and needs exactly the data it needed before, so the API surface is provably identical and the customer's QA surface is unchanged by construction. It is stronger than a sandbox because it is verifiable rather than argued — the platform's own engineers can confirm nothing was added or removed. It has never actually been checked, and building the gate against the existing app before promising it to anyone is a named open item.

**Delivery, as of the July 27 pivot, was a git branch on the customer's repo merged on their manual acceptance, with the divergence question deliberately deferred.** That was superseded the next day — see the addendum below, which also retired the SDK-shaped answer to integration. Integration into the customer's existing architecture was named as the biggest expected blocker, and the [[Concepts/Product/Mechanisms/Integration_Surface|Integration_Surface]] principle — adopt without changing your architecture — survives; what changed is that it is now satisfied by there being nothing to integrate rather than by a good SDK. Year one is forward-deployed: the founder does integration as paid onboarding, and per-tenant generation is near-zero marginal cost afterward.

**Why, in the pivot doc's order of weight.**

1. **Founder energy is binding and it points away from vertical business software.** *"I dont see myself doing crm long term, i wanna work with something more ai focused and cutting edge, if i end up just making busiess features i'll get bored, it was always a means to an end for me in the first place."* Clarified later: boring *work* is acceptable, but the core product must be an automation product. A solo founder without conviction does not make the hundredth cold call — the most economical explanation available for why the June 2 validation assignment sat unexecuted for eight weeks while build work continued.
2. **The engine is the strongest asset in the project and the only one that is genuinely scarce.** A working app-agnostic dependency DAG, **76** validated brand themes generated in under two hours (the pivot doc says 80; 76 is what is on disk and what `README.md` states), two production Flutter apps consuming it live, and a new app costing one folder rather than a code change. Nothing else in eight months is as differentiated.
3. **The strategic position is real, currently unoccupied, and the adjacent proof exists.** Token tooling stops at tokens; brand APIs and brand-kit generators stop before any app ships; design tools stop at a mockup a human still has to implement. No verified player closes brief → coherent multi-modal asset set → store-ready production app across a fleet of tenants ([[Concepts/Product/Mechanisms/Closed_Design_To_Production_Loop|Closed_Design_To_Production_Loop]]). Meanwhile Gigacatalyst (YC Spring 2026) has demonstrated the *functional* half of exactly this shape — per-tenant customization infrastructure embedded in a host SaaS, no changes to the host codebase, live with Series B companies — which validates the buyer, the motion and the architecture pattern without occupying the visual half.

**The name (July 28, 2026).** The company is named **Recompose**, chosen while drafting the YC founder video because a name has to be said out loud on camera. *Composition* is the design term for how elements are arranged on a screen, so *re-compose* names the arrangement-only invariant directly. Provisional and explicitly changeable — domain and trademark are unchecked. Rejected candidates and their reasoning are recorded in [[pivots/2026-07-27-11-customization-engine-is-the-company|pivots/2026-07-27-11-customization-engine-is-the-company.md]] (addendum) and [[YC_Application_F26|YC_Application_F26.md]].

**Pricing for this buyer is undefined.** The $100/$300/$500 ladder was gym-owner pricing and does not transfer. [[YC_Application_F26|YC_Application_F26.md]] states a planned **$100/month per branded tenant** as the answer to the revenue question, recorded there as a defensible anchor rather than a settled number; the office hours doc lists pricing as an open question and notes that "close enough at a fraction of the price" is meaningless until the fraction has a number.

### Phase 6 addendum — the layout half gets specified (July 28, 2026)

**The trigger was a test that failed.** The founder built structural preset enums and found they don't work, for a reason that generalizes: *"it's always going to be bounded by the design language originally... if it sticks to it it'll cease to be truly unique."* A bounded option set reproduces the platform's original design language however many options it exposes. That is a reskin — the exact dismissal this company exists to escape. **This is the first empirical finding in the project, and it came from building rather than reasoning.**

**What replaced it.** Free agentic editing, bounded by the invariant rather than by an enum. The platform's codebase is agentically tokenized, mirrored into a **feature-free JSX visual mock**, redesigned by an agent with full freedom, validated against **screenshots**, then converted back to production code by a **productionalize agent**. The mirror is visual only — no logic, no state, no API calls — which is what keeps the arrangement-only invariant enforceable on real production code where the platform's own engineers can still verify it by diff. Screenshots answer the accept-condition problem the July 27 pivot named as its largest remaining engineering risk.

**Recompose never writes to the platform's trunk.** This superseded the branch-and-merge delivery model within a day. Two fork layers, both agent-maintained: a shared tokenized fork as Recompose's base, re-derived against their main as they ship, and each tenant's design fork re-derived against that base. Changes surface in **UAT**, never straight to production. The platform grants read access; that is the entire integration. This kills the layout-enum model the founder had rated highest, because the enum requires writing generated layouts into their trunk. It also converts the largest named adoption blocker — *"we don't want an external system driving our production UI"* — from a technical objection into a non-issue, and the reason it works is organizational: write access needs engineering sign-off, read access does not.

**The promise got smaller and better — and then reversed two days later.** On July 28 it was *"We shouldn't be promising that agents are magic. We're just promising that one person can manage a hundred at a time,"* with the ~95/4/1 error budget acceptable **because** UAT and a human reviewer were the designed architecture. **Both halves of that fell on July 30** — see the addendum below.

**The unit of value moved with the pricing.** Charging per design rather than per tenant means revenue tracks how much design *variety* a platform buys, not how many tenants it has — a 500-tenant platform sharing three designs is a small account, a 50-tenant platform where everyone wants their own look is a large one. Premise P1 as written on 2026-07-27 is too blunt; tenant volume is a proxy that creates the possibility of variety rather than the value itself.

**Where the risk moved.** Not the mirror, which is cheap and amortized across every tenant. **Productionalize** is the hard part and the part that makes the product defensible. Its specific hazard: the mock has no edge cases, so the design agent designs against ideal data while production code is mostly edge cases. See [[pivots/2026-07-28-01-agentic-editing-all-in|pivots/2026-07-28-01-agentic-editing-all-in.md]] and [[Office_Hours/2026-07-28-01-Office_Hours_Agentic_Editing_Architecture|Office_Hours/2026-07-28-01-Office_Hours_Agentic_Editing_Architecture.md]].

**The open test — and it is the same hole as every prior phase.** *The engine has never been shown to a white-label platform.* There is no demand evidence for the new buyer, only reasoning. Nobody has asked for this; the origin is a realization while building a white-label app, not a request. No target platform and no named human at one has been produced yet — that was the single biggest hole the session found and it is unclosed. The validation instrument is untested: theming the example app live, in a sales call, against the prospect's own biggest customers, because the value claim is elapsed time and only a demo that runs in the room proves it. The assignment is ten named platforms with a named human at each, then three live-theming calls, before any more building.

**The countervailing risk, recorded and accepted rather than resolved.** This is the **sixth strategic phase in roughly eight months**. There are still **zero paying customers**. **No outside party has ever responded to anything built.** Each pivot was individually well-reasoned, which is exactly what makes this hard to see from inside — the risk is not bad reasoning, it is that reasoning has been the only instrument in use.

### Phase 6 addendum — the promise flips to autonomous (July 30, 2026)

**UAT turned out to be a branch, not a review environment.** A misreading of a branch name had been propagating across the vault: docs described UAT as a staging environment where a human worked an approval queue, and rested the error budget on that reviewer. There is no queue and there never was a decision to build one. Corrected across every doc that carried it.

**That removed the reviewer the promise was standing on — and the promise flipped rather than being patched.** The founder's position: human leverage was only ever the right framing while Recompose was going to edit the platform's *own* repository, where every change created review work for their engineers. On a Recompose-managed fork there is no PR and no queue on their side, so the constraint that produced the deflationary promise no longer exists. **The promise is now autonomous operation**, with the intended trend toward zero human involvement.

**What makes the error budget survivable is now halting, not reviewing.** An agent that cannot satisfy the accept condition stops and nothing deploys — a significant failure becomes a stall rather than an incident. The ~95/4/1 figure is also reclassified as a **target, never measured**. The 4% minor band is cosmetic by definition and does reach production, with nothing yet catching it.

**Two questions this opened, both live.** *Who resolves a halt* — leaning the platform's own staff, with a forward-deployed engineer considered and rejected-in-tension because an FDE lacks domain knowledge of an app they did not write. If the answer is platform staff, the demo's closing claim that there is "essentially no manual integration and maintenance" is false, and one of the two has to give. And *how the 4% gets swept up* — a scheduled code review was floated and is not a decision. See [[pivots/2026-07-30-22-autonomous-is-the-promise|pivots/2026-07-30-22-autonomous-is-the-promise.md]].

**The demo got recorded.** A 2:22 walkthrough built around a single worked example — one ballet studio carried through brief, generation, layout, animation and themes, then the platform's side. It is the first artifact that shows the whole loop rather than describing it. Still zero customers, still no platform has seen it.

### Phase 6 addendum — the wedge is re-evidenced and moves (August 2, 2026)

**The YC F26 application went in, and the next constraint was named as industry knowledge.** What began as migrating the useful research out of the two predecessor vaults became three commissioned research passes — design vs. feature work, build vs. buy, attach rate — plus live verification against vendor pages, SEC filings and job postings. The product did not change. The evidence under the value argument did, and three things the vault had treated as settled turned out to be wrong.

**The design-service P&L line does not exist.** P1a had held that a platform running a design service can read the value off a headcount line. Circle's design department is ~4 people out of ~160 and its own postings describe it as the internal *marketing* design team; Vagaro's service is a $499 flat fee; ABC Trainerize prices the same class of deliverable standalone at $99–169 one-time; professional services runs 1.19% of revenue at Olo and 2.9% at Toast. **Cost reduction is dead as a pitch — there is not enough cost to save.** What survives is the 3–6 week human turnaround, consistent everywhere it is published, which is a capacity ceiling rather than a cost line.

**The premium tier price is not a design price.** Mighty Pro's tier lists six deliverables and design is one; the named human attached to it is a launch project manager and business coach whose published responsibilities are workshops, content cadence, member onboarding flows and email sequences. The tier is priced on the size of the tenant's business. Recompose replaces one bullet of six.

**Attach rate was the question nobody had asked, and it reframes the ICP.** Measured on Shopify — the only vertical where it can be — mobile-app-builder adoption is **1.73% of Plus stores and 0.10% overall**, and it tracks **tenant size** across at least a 70× range at one price. So a shell-model platform's tenant count is roughly a hundred times its addressable app count. The filter moves from *"already runs a design service"* to **"every tenant already has their own app"** — Subsplash, Passion.io, Q2, Banno, Alkami — where the two numbers are the same. Subsplash fits best: 20,000+ tenants each with an app, no regulatory pressure toward visual sameness, and a former customer on record leaving over customization limits. See [[pivots/2026-08-02-23-icp-filter-moves-to-tenants-who-already-have-apps|pivots/2026-08-02-23-icp-filter-moves-to-tenants-who-already-have-apps.md]].

**Two findings cut the other way and are worth holding onto.** [[Concepts/Product/Mechanisms/No_New_Requests_Invariant|No_New_Requests_Invariant]] got **stronger**: 13 of 19 platforms researched have no path by which a tenant gets net-new functionality at any price, so "no new features" is the answer the platform already gives its own tenants rather than a constraint Recompose imposes. And the white-label layer is durable — Red Lobster left Olo, built in-house, and came back in 2025; Bloomin' Brands, Brinker and Papa Murphy's all went homegrown → Olo.

**[[Concepts/Business/Strategy/Brand_Is_Product|Brand_Is_Product]] took its third independent hit.** Across a dozen documented custom-build cases, design has **never** been the sole reason anyone built bespoke. That joins the two May 2026 findings — design labor was never the most binding constraint, and app-store reviews complain about bugs, never about looking generic. The counter the session held: going bespoke is a six-figure decision nobody makes for design alone, and Recompose enables a different one. Nothing has tested that. **Whether attach rate responds to design quality at all is now the largest open question in the vault (T19), and no amount of further research can settle it.**

## Key structural lessons from the whole arc

1. **Cold CRM *replacement* is dead — becoming the operational layer is not.** Walking into a gym that already runs on PushPress/Mindbody and asking it to switch its billing fails on migration friction; that's structural, and Phase 1 proved it. But that is *cold rip-and-replace*, not the long-term thesis. Becoming the operational/CRM layer via **land-and-expand** — win gyms with the engagement/brand wedge, own the front-of-house member relationship, then consolidate the back office onto you — is the documented direction ([[Architecture_Thesis|Architecture_Thesis.md]], `CombatDen:Future_Ideas_and_Explorations/future_vision.md`). **Not now, but for the future, once the core engagement product is validated, built, and shipped.** The earlier "don't try again" framing over-generalized the cold-replacement failure into a permanent law and contradicted the architecture thesis; corrected 2026-06-01 (see `CombatDen:Business/pivots/2026-06-01-14-beachhead-unlimited-membership-crm-as-future-layer.md`). **Update 2026-06-02:** the project now pursues the CRM directly via a third path — not cold rip-and-replace, and not deferred land-and-expand, but *replacement targeted only at gyms whose incumbent CRM is switch-friendly,* where migration friction is low by construction. See `CombatDen:Business/pivots/2026-06-02-19-return-to-full-crm-switch-friendly-beachhead.md`.
2. **Layered architecture has a real value-add ceiling.** Coach-dependent features are structurally hard without an adoption hook the layered app doesn't own. The ceiling was diagnosed explicitly and accepted — the product works within it.
3. **Features added from thinness anxiety don't survive first principles.** Messaging was added and cut same-day precisely because the discipline held.
4. **The unit economics had to be fixed, not just the product.** The merge solved a structural problem that better retention software alone couldn't fix.
5. **The design bet needs to actually be miles better, not marginally better.** The whole thesis rests on the orchestrator producing first-pass output that makes a buyer go "whoa" in 5 seconds. That's the engineering bar, and it's unvalidated against real gyms at scale.

### What the whole arc teaches Recompose (added 2026-07-28)

6. **Reasoning has been the only instrument, and that is the actual failure pattern.** Six phases, roughly eight months, zero paying customers, and no outside party has ever responded to anything built. Every phase cleared an intellectual bar and none cleared a market one. Whether Phase 6 becomes the sixth phase or the last one is decided entirely by whether the outreach happens before any more code does.
7. **Build-instead-of-sell is how each phase actually ended.** The June 2 validation assignment sat unexecuted for eight weeks while rewards productionization, the member app, and Kiosk Mode all shipped. Each of those was good work. None of them tested demand. The same trap is available in Phase 6 — the engine is fun to build and the ten-named-platforms list is not.
8. **Founder conviction is a hard constraint, not a soft preference — and the filter is sharper than "boring vs. fun."** The CRM was not abandoned at a low point. It was **fully built, and the founder was roughly a week from taking it to customers** when he stopped. Not from avoidance — from comparison. Working on the customization engine is what exposed the indifference: *"I was legit a week from going to customers, I had the whole thing built, but I realized I really don't care about it, and this idea is what made me realize that."*

    His own statement of the ceiling: *"I would've sold off the company in a year of it being successful, because managing a business or making software to manually make a business good is so boring to me."* A founder who has priced the exit before the first sale has already priced his own conviction.

    **The filter, in his words: boring is fine if the base of what you're building is fun and impactful.** The test is not how much of the work is tedious — it is *where the interesting thing sits relative to what the buyer is actually buying.* At CombatDen the video engine and the theme service were the parts he cared about, and neither was the main selling point, so the boring work only led to more boring work. The acceptable shape is inverted: *"get through the boring to make the fun vision come true, instead of get past the boring to have a little fun on the side."* Run this over every future idea **before** the market analysis, not after it.
9. **The one durable asset produced in eight months was infrastructure, not product.** Five gym-facing product theses were discarded; the app-agnostic generation pipeline survived all of them and became the company. When output is discarded that fast, the thing worth noticing is what kept getting reused.
10. **Take the ambition that raises scope without raising risk.** Phase 6 went from theming to a full visual redesign including layout — a materially bigger claim — while simultaneously getting a *tighter* safety story, because arrangement-only functional equivalence is verifiable by the customer's own engineers. Most scope increases trade safety away. The ones that don't are the credible ones.

## CombatDen build milestones (now the reference implementation)

*These shipped as the gym product. They are retained here as the build record of what is now Recompose's reference implementation and demo canvas — not as current company direction.*

**Rewards system productionized (June 28, 2026).** The loyalty / rewards stack moved from prototype to real production infrastructure. Points now have two live earning paths: attendance auto-award (check-in writer reads `gym_classes.points_worth`) and manual staff award/adjustment from the CRM. Redemptions have a real approval flow: members initiate a `pending` request; staff approve or reject from a CRM approval queue; a rejection automatically refunds the points. Staff can also redeem on behalf of a member (auto-approved, with an override to drain to zero). The CRM's Loyalty tab is now a full bloc-backed admin surface (reward catalog CRUD with image upload + point costs, approval queue) — a deliberate exception to the member-app-preview tabs being stateless. Image uploads for reward catalog images and member photos flow through a new `uploads` domain in the FastApiBackend (multipart S3 proxy → `cdn.combatden.net`), reusing the same `combatden-assets` bucket ThemeService uses.

**Member app + Kiosk Mode go live (July 23, 2026).** Two surfaces launch together as the retention layer:

- **Member App** is now the live client of the FastApiBackend **member portal** (`src/member_portal/`), with real Supabase auth and every screen wired to `/api/v1/member/...` The look of the existing screens was preserved, real member data went live under them. A member signs in with the email their gym has on file (self-signup + email confirmation — the same verified-email identity the staff app uses; **no invite-token flow**, matching the existing design), picks their profile when a shared family email resolves to several members, and lands on a home screen re-themed to their gym, showing real streak, points, rank, schedule, reservations, rewards, and a personalized video feed. They can reserve/cancel classes and request reward redemptions; when staff check them in, the next app open plays a post-class celebration with the real numbers. A QR check-in scanner surface is in place as the seam the parallel Kiosk workstream's real scan contract will plug into. Draft PR #60. **Deliberately deferred to fast-follow PRs:** member invite emails (Supabase invite link) and push notifications (reservation reminders + an after-class nudge) — the two things that actually pull a member back into the app. The theme selector was removed from the member app. See `CombatDen:Business/pivots/2026-07-23-03-mobileapp-productized-member-app.md`.

- **Kiosk Mode** is the gym's front-desk member surface — an iPad-first, staff-controlled entry point running inside the authenticated CRM. It has two member flows: (1) **check-in glance** — existing members find their name, see their streak/points/rewards tier, get nudged toward the app via a "Get the app" modal, then the screen resets for the next person; (2) **self-serve signup** — new members or existing members without a current membership walk a structured solo/group lane (details → plan → waiver → card → review → pay → results receipt) with two critical rules baked in: the **fresh-card law** (every card entered becomes the payer's default, replacing pre-existing cards) and the **one-trial rule** (a member's first trial locks them out of other trial plans). The kiosk lives in `codebase/CRM/lib/features/kiosk/`, boots with a 12-hour absolute server-anchored runway (lockout at T+11h45, hard revoke at T+12h), and has its own security model (session inside authenticated app, dyadic kiosk exit = app sign-out). Members who sign up on the kiosk land on the same app-adoption surface as check-in glance — both push the "Get the app" modal. The kiosk's full design doc is the **kiosk-guide skill** in the worktree. See `CombatDen:Business/pivots/2026-07-23-03-mobileapp-productized-member-app.md`.
