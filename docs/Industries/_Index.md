> [!warning] Reasoned, not validated
> No platform in any of these files has been contacted. The scores are a structured argument about where design demand *should* concentrate, not a measurement of it. The vault's largest open question (T19 — whether attach rate responds to design quality at all) is unresolved and this ranking cannot settle it.

## What is being scored

**Design demand from the tenant's side** — does the tenant's visual identity carry commercial weight, and do tenants need to look different from each other. Five criteria, 1–5 each:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Brand-load** | Is the tenant's visual identity commercially load-bearing? |
| **Variety** | How different do tenants need to look *from each other*? This is the unit of value — Recompose sells designs, not tenants. |
| **Permission** | Freedom from regulatory, trust or accessibility pressure toward sameness. |
| **End-user stakes** | Does an end user compare, choose, or convert on the look? |
| **Pay** | Tenant scale and realistic budget for a design. |

**Addressability is scored separately and deliberately.** The no-shell count is metadata on each file, not part of the score, because the two questions are independent — and the most useful findings come from where they disagree. Ties in score are broken by no-shell density.

## What falls out of it

**The current shortlist is inverted on this axis.** Three of the five named ICP candidates — Q2, Banno, Alkami — are banking, ranked **16 of 21**. They qualified on addressability and nothing else. Banking scores 5/5 on ability to pay and 1/5 on wanting variety: the vertical most able to buy and least willing to want.

**The top-ranked vertical is 6-for-6 no-shell and is on no list.** Ecommerce app builders pass both filters simultaneously, and are the only place design ROI is measurable (conversion rate) — which makes them the natural venue for T19. The counter is real: their tenants are already the self-selected 1.73%, and Tapcart-class builders ship drag-drop layout editors.

**Addressability alone produces bad targets.** Property management (17th) and education (20th) are near-perfectly addressable — 6/6 and 3/4 no-shell at 7.8M units and 60M+ students — with essentially no design demand under them. Passing the no-shell test is necessary and nowhere near sufficient.

**Subsplash survives on volume, not design.** Church ranks 11th. The case is 20,000+ tenants each already holding an app plus a churned customer on record; it is not a design-demand case, and it should not be pitched as one.

**Events has the highest design-per-tenant multiplier.** Every conference is a new brand campaign, so one tenant buys designs repeatedly — the only vertical where that is true, and the cleanest fit to selling designs rather than tenants. Blocked by the model: only EventMobi is no-shell.

**A hypothesis worth testing, not a finding.** Verticals where design matters most are the likeliest to have already shipped self-serve customization, because demand pulled it out of them; verticals where it matters least never got asked. If it holds, the sweet spot is narrow — high design demand, no builder yet — pointing at hotels, golf/clubs and salon/spa. It is not clean: Alkami has a Theme Builder while sitting near the bottom, because compliance drove it rather than demand.
