# What Businesses Needed That Their White-Label / Vertical SaaS Platform Could Not Give Them

## 1. TL;DR
- Across the documented build/hybrid cases assembled here, the problem surface is not concentrated in the end-customer mobile app. Of the 6 named companies that built or hybridized their own product (Compass, Red Lobster, Wingstop, MSUFCU, UW Credit Union, Lumino Studios), the recurring underlying driver is DATA/API (first-party data ownership, integration, export) and BACK OFFICE/OPS (ordering, catering, entitlement logic); the mobile app is the visible surface in 2 cases (Compass, Lumino) but rarely the sole driver.
- The single most cross-cutting missing capability revealed by platform marketplaces is accounting/ERP/financial sync (QuickBooks, NetSuite, Sage): it is a named third-party category on Shopify, ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Clio, Mindbody and Toast. Custom reporting/BI dashboards and reviews/ratings are the next most repeated. There is a repeating shortlist of roughly 8–10 capability clusters plus a genuinely long tail — StoreCensus reports "the top 100 apps account for just 34% of total installs" on Shopify.
- One case is a full round trip and it dead-ends at the artifact level: Red Lobster left Olo in 2023 for homegrown digital ordering and returned in 2025. No published source states, feature by feature, what the homegrown system did that Olo did not — that artifact is UNKNOWN. Olo's June 26, 2025 release states the return was made "citing the platform's enhanced capabilities, cost-effectiveness, and ability to keep pace with evolving technology trends."

## 2. Key Findings

1. **Surface distribution: mobile app is not the dominant driver.** In the 6 documented custom/hybrid builds, DATA/API and BACK OFFICE/OPS drivers outnumber pure MOBILE APP drivers. MOBILE APP is the visible surface in Compass and Lumino; DATA/API is the driver in Wingstop (first-party data), UW Credit Union (data integration) and MSUFCU (online banking/data); BACK OFFICE/OPS is the driver in Red Lobster (digital ordering + first-party catering). Denominator = 6 cases. (MEASURED for existence of the builds via press; ESTIMATED for the surface split, which is my classification of 6 cases.) Because the requester specifically wanted to know whether pain is elsewhere than mobile: in this sample it is, clustering in data ownership and back-office/ops logic.

2. **Lumino Studios / bsport is the most feature-specific single case.** The agency that built it (Brocoders) lists the exact capabilities bsport's API could not expose: "User progress tracking," "Advanced analytics," "Coach reviews (which they use for performance evaluations and impact on the coach's salary)," and "Class ratings," plus "open access control" for "multi-zone locations." Brocoders states bsport "changed an internal endpoint — no changelog, no warning — and broke part of our integration," and that they built by "reverse-engineering bsport's public web version to extract the data we needed." Surface: MOBILE APP + DATA/API. (VENDOR-CLAIMED — the builder's own case study; "Lumino Studios" is stated in the post to be a pseudonym.)

3. **The coach-reviews-tied-to-payroll feature in the Lumino case is irreducibly per-tenant.** Ratings feeding directly into an individual coach's salary calculation is specific to how that one operator runs compensation; no fitness platform would ship that logic to its whole base. Access control, by contrast, is a ROADMAP GAP that bsport has since addressed through a partner integration (Kisi "click-to-open" door unlock via the bsport app, documented on Kisi's site). (VENDOR-CLAIMED for the feature; MEASURED for the Kisi integration's existence via Kisi docs.)

4. **Red Lobster round trip — artifact UNKNOWN.** Red Lobster used Olo 2017–2023, left in 2023 to build homegrown online ordering, and returned June 26, 2025 with an expanded partnership adding Olo Sentiment (reputation management) and Catering+ (first-party catering, "for the first time in Red Lobster's history"). Per Olo's BusinessWire release, Red Lobster rejoined "citing the platform's enhanced capabilities, cost-effectiveness, and ability to keep pace with evolving technology trends," after departing in 2023 to build homegrown ordering; CMO Nichole Robillard: "While we gained valuable learning from our internal development efforts, we're thrilled to be partnering with Olo again." The homegrown system's feature list versus Olo's was not published; that it was replaced by Olo is documented, but its distinguishing features are UNKNOWN. (MEASURED via Olo/BusinessWire release and Restaurant Business reporting.)

5. **Wingstop (MyWingstop) is a data-control build, not primarily a mobile-app build.** Wingstop announced it would leave Olo for a proprietary $50 million platform whose stated purpose is first-party data capture and "hyper-personalization." CEO Michael Skipworth said MyWingstop "serves the dual purpose of protecting the brand's digital business, which now accounts for $2 billion in sales" (Restaurant Dive, Q3 2023 call); Wingstop had $1.7B in digital sales in 2022, rising past $2B over the following 12 months (Tasting Table). Olo CEO Noah Glass, on the same event, said: "Olo spends $90 million annually to maintain our platform… That is a case study in the economies of scale of a SaaS platform. That's not a belief. That's not even a judgment. That's just math." (Restaurant Business). Surface: DATA/API (first-party data) + MOBILE APP (ordering). Driver: control/data. (MEASURED via QSR/NRN/Restaurant Business/Restaurant Dive reporting of earnings calls; the $50M figure is company-stated, so VENDOR-CLAIMED for the amount.)

6. **MSUFCU is on record that the driver was vendor pace (a roadmap-gap framing).** Benjamin Maxim (chief digital strategy and innovation officer) is quoted: vendors "were just not keeping up with what we needed to do and not really keeping up with how the internet and the web was evolving, so we built our own apps, our own websites and our own online bank." MSUFCU then partnered with CU NextGen to build a new digital banking platform, "Nextly." This is a ROADMAP GAP framing (slow vendor) rather than an irreducibly per-tenant need. Surface: DATA/API + WEB + MOBILE APP. (MEASURED — direct attributed quote in CU Management, April 2023.)

7. **UW Credit Union — driver is data/integration; specific artifact largely UNKNOWN.** UWCU's public record shows a CIO (Ralph Buelling, hired Dec 2021) leading "data analytics, and e-commerce technology initiatives" and "data integration," and a VP of Data Analytics (Nishant Upadhyay) presenting on "Data Organizing Principles." The specific custom-built feature is not published at artifact level. Surface: DATA/API. (MEASURED for roles/quotes via UWCU press and CULytics; ARTIFACT UNKNOWN for the specific build.)

8. **Compass built three native apps; the driver is control of the agent-client workflow and proprietary data.** Compass's own engineering blog (Compass True North, Medium) states: "We currently offer three different iOS apps" — the consumer Compass app; "Compass Markets" (a real-time market-trends/valuation app); and an agent-only open-house check-in app that lets agents "record every potential consumer who checks in to their open house." Compass also built a proprietary CRM, Marketing Center, pricing tools, and a data warehouse stack (Redshift/Snowflake/Tableau/Looker). In July 2026 Compass bundled these into a "Home Platform." Surface: MOBILE APP + DATA/API. Classification: control/data → the exclusive-data and agent-client-workflow rationale reads as CATEGORY REFUSAL/per-tenant (Compass will not rent its lead and client-interaction data to a third-party vendor). (MEASURED via Compass engineering blog and Inman.)

9. **Marketplace readout: accounting/ERP sync is the most universal missing capability.** It appears as a named third-party category or top integration on Shopify (QuickBooks/accounting), ServiceTitan (accounting is a named category; Agave/Acumatica, Sage Intacct), Jobber (QuickBooks is the headline integration), Housecall Pro (QuickBooks Online most-cited), Clio (QuickBooks/Xero), Mindbody (Xero/QuickBooks), and Toast (QuickBooks via Shogo/native). Each is a capability the core platform did not natively fully provide and that customers pay a third party for. (MEASURED for existence of the listings; these are listing counts / review-cited integrations, which measure demand-at-listing, not usage.)

10. **The repeating shortlist is real; so is the long tail.** Aggregating cases + marketplaces, ~8–10 capability clusters recur across verticals (accounting/ERP sync; custom reporting/BI; reviews/ratings/reputation; marketing/email-SMS automation; first-party/customer-data ownership; payments/financing; branded/custom mobile UX; scheduling/dispatch custom logic; inventory/WMS; access control). StoreCensus, tracking 2.5M+ stores, reports "The long tail is real. The top 100 apps account for just 34% of total installs—thousands of niche apps serve specialized vertical needs." (MEASURED for the Shopify concentration figure via StoreCensus; ESTIMATED for the cross-vertical shortlist, which is my aggregation.)

11. **Consultant/agency services pages are the clearest "refused-request lists."** Bolder Apps advertises building, for ServiceTitan operators, "Custom dispatch tools built for unique business models that ServiceTitan's default scheduling can't accommodate," "Customer-facing portals that pull live ServiceTitan data — job status, invoices, booking, and service history in one place," connectors to "legacy ERPs, and tools your finance team can't give up," and "Simplified mobile apps for field techs — faster than the ServiceTitan app." TitanSigma states: "ServiceTitan does not have a ready-made integration for Power BI, but TitanSigma's zero-ETL integration between the two platforms fills the void." For Shopify Plus, Folio3 lists "custom checkout logic," "Integrate ERP, PIM, CRM, WMS, 3PL," and B2B "company accounts with approval flows … PO-based ordering"; Liquify lists "calculators, VAT receipts, apps for warehouse management, realtime api feeds." For Mindbody, APIANT lists named connectors (ShopConnect for Shopify commerce/inventory sync; CRMConnect for Zoho, syncing "memberships with expiration dates and remaining sessions, contracts, auto-pay status"). (VENDOR-CLAIMED — agencies' own services pages; evidence of demand-at-a-price, not delivered volume.)

12. **Vertical-level null results.** For churches/faith, the incumbent (Subsplash) has absorbed custom needs into its own product (ChMS, branded apps, giving, AI tools), and no documented case of a church leaving to build its own app was found. For veterinary, franchise (as a standalone vertical distinct from restaurants), education, hospitality (distinct from restaurants), fleet/logistics, community platforms and events, no attributable custom-build artifact was found in the sources searched. These are NEGATIVE RESULTS, detailed in Section 5.

## 3. Details by research question and population

### 3.1 Built custom (left the platform or built alongside)
These are the biggest companies with the highest willingness to pay — the least representative population, per the brief's own framing.

- **Compass (real estate) — BUILT CUSTOM.** Artifact: three native iOS apps (consumer Compass app; Compass Markets real-time valuation app; agent-only open-house check-in that records attendee leads), plus proprietary CRM, Marketing Center, pricing tools, and a data warehouse (Redshift/Snowflake/Tableau/Looker). In July 2026 Compass rolled these into a bundled "Home Platform" (CMA, Collections, Buyer Demand pricing intelligence, reverse prospecting, agent-to-agent search network, marketing center, live listing dashboard). Surface: MOBILE APP + DATA/API. Roadmap-gap vs per-tenant: CATEGORY REFUSAL / per-tenant — Compass frames its exclusive inventory and agent-client interaction data as "a structural advantage" it will not source from a vendor. Platforms it displaced (kvCORE, BoomTown, Follow Up Boss, dotloop, Skyslope) publish overlapping feature lists, so parts (CRM, marketing center) are ROADMAP GAP relative to those vendors; the data-ownership rationale is not. Source: Compass True North (Medium), Compass.com, Inman (2026), Monte Carlo blog. Evidence quality: primary (engineering blog) + third-party (Inman).

- **Red Lobster (restaurants) — BUILT CUSTOM then reverted (round trip).** Artifact: ARTIFACT UNKNOWN at feature level. Homegrown digital ordering (2023) → back to Olo (June 2025), adopting Sentiment and Catering+ (first-party catering, a new capability). Surface: BACK OFFICE/OPS + DATA/API. Roadmap-gap vs per-tenant: the return itself shows the homegrown capabilities were reproducible by a vendor (ROADMAP GAP), but the distinguishing features are unpublished. Source: BusinessWire/Olo IR (2025-06-26), Restaurant Business, FSR magazine, Food On Demand. Evidence quality: primary (press release) + third-party.

- **Wingstop (restaurants, franchise) — BUILT CUSTOM.** Artifact: MyWingstop proprietary ordering + CRM stack; stated purpose first-party data capture and hyper-personalization; $50M stated investment; rollout began April 2024; the platform "has been tested in about 150 of the company's … nearly 2,000 U.S. restaurants" (NRN, quoting the Q4 2023 call). MyWingstop's database stood at ~40 million users at the April 2024 systemwide rollout (NRN) and "now encompasses 60 million guests" as of the Q2 2025 call (Restaurant Dive). Surface: DATA/API + MOBILE APP. Roadmap-gap vs per-tenant: control/data — the capability (personalization on first-party data) is one Olo also markets, so functionally a ROADMAP GAP; the motive is data ownership. Source: QSR Magazine, NRN, Restaurant Business, Restaurant Dive, Tasting Table. Evidence quality: third-party press reporting company earnings calls; $50M is VENDOR-CLAIMED.

- **MSUFCU (credit union) — BUILT CUSTOM then partnered.** Artifact: own apps, websites, online banking; then "Nextly" digital banking platform via CU NextGen; and a gamified "Game Day Experience" — per CU Management, MSUFCU "invited 30,000 members to sign up to play Game Day Experience, and about 5,000 joined in," partnering with Flow Networks and tied to MSU football games (MSUFCU is described as a $7.3 billion, 338,000-member credit union). Surface: DATA/API + WEB + MOBILE APP. Roadmap-gap vs per-tenant: ROADMAP GAP (stated driver = vendors "not keeping up"). Source: CU Management (April 2023). Evidence quality: primary (attributed executive quotes).

- **UW Credit Union (credit union) — BUILT/INTEGRATED.** Artifact: ARTIFACT UNKNOWN specifically; documented investment in data analytics, data integration, e-commerce. Surface: DATA/API. Source: UWCU press, CULytics. Evidence quality: primary for roles; artifact unknown.

- **Bloomin' Brands, Brinker/Chili's, Papa Murphy's — homegrown → Olo (adopted vendor).** These run the reverse direction. Chili's/Brinker adopted Olo in 2016 for pre-order, group order, payment flexibility, and loyalty-point payment via My Chili's Rewards; Papa Murphy's launched a new site + Olo ordering (with texts on pickup readiness and planned half-and-half pizza ordering, plus streamlined franchisee payments). What their prior homegrown systems did versus Olo is not published at feature level (ARTIFACT UNKNOWN for the homegrown side). Surface: BACK OFFICE/OPS. Source: PRNewswire/Brinker IR (2016), Pizza Marketplace, SEC 8-K (Papa Murphy's, FRSH). Evidence quality: primary press.

### 3.2 Bought a third-party app or integration (marketplace demand revealed at a price)
See Section 4 tables. Key qualitative readouts:
- **Shopify:** top categories by app count are Shipping Labels, Analytics, Discounts (Meetanshi); top by review volume are Product Reviews, SEO, Popups. Most-installed apps by store count are review and email/SMS tools (Judge.me, Klaviyo, Shopify Inbox). Review counts are published; install counts are not (only third-party trackers like StoreCensus/StoreLeads estimate them).
- **ServiceTitan:** 140+ certified integrations across 26 categories; Marketing is the largest single category (30+ partners); accounting, payments/financing, HR/payroll, fleet/GPS, inventory, and dispatching are named categories.
- **Jobber:** surpassed 100 integrations (May 2026); QuickBooks is the headline; a documented recurring gap is per-photo GPS-tagging / required-photo checklists / PDF handover reports, which third parties (Manifold) fill.
- **Clio:** 250+ apps, described by Clio as "legal's largest app ecosystem"; QuickBooks/Xero (accounting), LawPay (payments/trust accounting), intake/AI receptionist.
- **Mindbody, Toast, Housecall Pro:** accounting/payments/marketing dominate; see tables.

### 3.3 Duct-taped it (Zapier / Make / n8n)
- **Zapier does NOT publish per-template usage counts.** Zapier's docs define how "tasks" are counted for billing but publish no per-Zap or per-template public usage ranking; template listings show ordering/curation, not usage. Any claim that "the most-used Zapier template per platform" is publicly readable is not supported. (NEGATIVE RESULT.)
- **n8n publishes a per-template `totalViews` field** (visible via its templates API and on template pages), so template views (not deployments) are available. n8n's own library size is vendor-reported in a range ("800 to 900-plus templates" per one methodology-noting source), while a separate SEO source claims "9,400 community-built workflows" — these do not reconcile (see Section 6). Views measure interest, not usage.
- The field-service and fitness platforms (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Mindbody) expose Zapier/Make triggers around the same primitives: new client, new/updated job, new invoice, new quote, new booking — indicating the duct-tape demand centers on syncing these records outward to other systems (a DATA/API surface).

## 5. What the evidence does and does not support

**Established (MEASURED or strongly documented):**
- Red Lobster's departure (2023) and return (2025) to Olo, with the added Sentiment and first-party Catering+ scope, is documented in a primary press release and corroborated by trade press.
- The bsport/Lumino API gaps are documented in verbatim detail by the builder (progress tracking, coach reviews tied to salary, class ratings, multi-zone access control, endpoint instability).
- Accounting/ERP sync is the most cross-cutting third-party category across seven named platform marketplaces.
- On Shopify, review counts are published and install counts are not; per StoreCensus the top 100 apps account for 34% of installs, evidencing a long tail.
- MSUFCU's stated driver ("not keeping up") is an attributed, dated executive quote.
- Wingstop's MyWingstop is a stated first-party-data play (protecting a $2B digital business; a database that grew from ~40M to ~60M guests), corroborated across QSR/NRN/Restaurant Dive.

**Open (unresolved facts / open questions the search did not close):**
- What, feature by feature, Red Lobster's homegrown 2023 ordering system did that Olo did not — UNKNOWN in the public record. Searched: Olo IR/press, BusinessWire, Restaurant Business, FSR, Food On Demand.
- The specific artifact UW Credit Union built — UNKNOWN. Only roles and thematic priorities are public.
- Total app counts for Mindbody's integrations directory — no public number located.
- Whether the homegrown pre-Olo systems at Brinker/Papa Murphy's had distinguishing features — UNKNOWN.
- Precise, audited per-tenant vs roadmap-gap proportions cannot be computed to a hard denominator. My classification of the 6 build cases yields roughly: roadmap gap 3 (Red Lobster, MSUFCU, Wingstop-functionally); per-tenant/category-refusal 2 (Compass, Lumino's payroll-linked reviews); unclassified 1 (UWCU). Small sample; treat as ESTIMATED.

**Facts that would most reorder the findings (open questions, not advice):**
- If Red Lobster or UW Credit Union published a feature-level teardown of their homegrown builds, the surface distribution and the roadmap-gap/per-tenant split could shift materially. This is the single largest missing datapoint.
- If Mindbody or another fitness platform published marketplace install counts, the marketplace readout would move from listing-volume (demand-at-listing) to usage.
- If a platform published Zapier/Make deployment (not view) counts per template, the duct-tape long tail could be ranked by usage rather than existence.

**Negative results (searched and not found):**
- Churches/faith: no documented case of a church leaving a white-label platform to build its own app; Subsplash appears to have absorbed the demand into its own product (ChMS, branded apps, giving, AI tools). Searched: Subsplash product pages, church-app comparison sites.
- Veterinary, education, hospitality (distinct from restaurants), fleet/logistics, community platforms, events, franchise (as a standalone vertical): no attributable artifact-level custom-build case found in sources searched.
- Salon/spa, property management, healthcare/dental: no artifact-level custom-build case surfaced in this pass (marketplace-level demand exists via Vagaro/Zenoti but specific counts were not retrievable within budget — NEGATIVE RESULT on counts).

## 6. Circulating statistics that do not survive sourcing
- **"MyWingstop cost $100 million."** Appears in SEO blogs (matrixbcg.com, businessmodelcanvastemplate.com). The figure sourced to Wingstop earnings calls and reported by QSR Magazine, NRN and Restaurant Business is **$50 million**. The $100M figure dead-ends in unsourced marketing blogs; the $50M figure traces to company statements. Conflict, not average.
- **n8n template library size.** "9,400 community-built workflows" (connectsafely.ai) vs vendor-reported "800 to 900-plus templates" (techjacksolutions.com, which flags it as a vendor range). These do not reconcile and likely count different populations (community submissions vs curated library). Both dead-end at vendor/SEO assertions.
- **"87% of Shopify merchants use apps; average 6 apps."** Widely repeated (uptek.com, kunalm substack) and traceable to ~2018-era Shopify statements. StoreCensus's 2026 tracking says the average is 5.9 apps and 18% of stores use zero third-party apps. The "87%" figure is stale and not re-verified.
- **Shopify total app count.** Ranges from 8,659 (StoreCensus) to 12,320 (Meetanshi, Jan 2025) to ~17,600 (craftberry, Q1 2026) to 27,523 (StoreLeads). These measure different things (actively tracked listed apps vs all listed vs listed+historical/unlisted). No single audited number exists.
- **Mindbody "customers who use integrations earn 10% more revenue, 40% more bookings, ~35% more client purchases."** VENDOR-CLAIMED on Mindbody's own integrations site; no methodology, sample size, or control published. The source does not establish causation.

## 7. Caveats
- **Sample size.** The custom/hybrid build set is 6 named companies plus 2 vendor-adoption cases — small, skewed to large enterprises (per the brief, the least representative population). Proportions (surface split, per-tenant vs roadmap gap) are computed on this small n and are ESTIMATED, not audited.
- **What the marketplace numbers measure.** App counts per category measure listing volume, not usage. Shopify review counts are not install counts. ServiceTitan/Jobber/Clio integration counts are certified-listing counts, not adoption. Zapier template listings are ordering/curation, not usage, and Zapier publishes no per-template usage. n8n publishes template views, which are not deployments. These distinctions are stated at each point of use above.
- **Vendor bias.** The Lumino case is authored by the agency that built it and paid to publicize it (Brocoders); "Lumino Studios" is an explicit pseudonym, so the case cannot be independently verified against a named company. Wingstop's $50M and Mindbody's uplift figures are company-stated. Consultant services-page quotes (Bolder Apps, Folio3, Liquify, APIANT, Peaka, TitanSigma) are marketing copy describing what they sell — evidence of demand-at-a-price but not of delivered volume; APIANT's testimonial block contains placeholder/dummy names and was treated as unreliable for testimonials while its product descriptions were used.
- **Read-across.** The restaurant "build vs buy" cases (Red Lobster, Wingstop, Brinker, Papa Murphy's) are drawn heavily from Olo's own ecosystem, which has an interest in framing homegrown builds as failures; Olo's cost claim ($90M/year across 600 brands) is from Olo's CEO.
- **Recency.** Marketplace counts range from Jan 2025 to Q2 2026 and move quickly; the Compass "Home Platform" bundling is dated July 2026. Figures are as of the cited dates.
- **Computed vs reported.** The ranked missing-capability list and the surface distribution are my aggregations across heterogeneous sources, not a single reported dataset.
