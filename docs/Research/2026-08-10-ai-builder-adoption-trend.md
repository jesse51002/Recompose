# The Adoption Trend for AI Website Generators: Growth Rate, Segments, and Motivation

## TL;DR
- **The category is growing explosively in revenue, but the leading edge — standalone "vibe-coding" generators — hit a demand inflection in mid-2025: ARR is still compounding while top-of-funnel traffic rolled over hard (Lovable −40%, Vercel v0 −64%, Bolt.new −27% from spring/summer 2025 peaks, per a Barclays note reported Sep 26, 2025).** The *durable* growth has shifted to AI *embedded inside incumbents* (GoDaddy Airo, Squarespace Blueprint, Wix Base44/Harmony), which is the channel actually reaching non-technical owners.
- **Growth is segment-specific, not uniform.** Solo founders/indie hackers and developers drove the 2024–2025 spike (among the fastest ARR ramps in software history); non-technical SMB adoption is real but concentrated in incumbents' onboarding, not standalone SMB tools (Durable/B12/Mixo, which publish only vanity "sites built" counts); enterprise is the newest and stickiest wedge.
- **The single most important finding: the curve is bifurcating — steepening on revenue/enterprise, flattening or rolling over on consumer/prototyper traffic and interest.** Non-adoption is dominated by *no need* and *unawareness*, not active rejection; but a genuine "vibe-coding backlash" (security incidents, a collapse in developer trust, a paid agency-cleanup market) emerged in 2025–2026 and is a real drag.

## Key Findings
1. **Revenue growth is real and among the fastest ever recorded — but almost entirely usage/credit-driven, not seat-driven.** Lovable went $100M ARR (Jun/Jul 2025) → $200M (Nov 2025) → $400M (~Feb 2026) → $500M (Jun 2026). Bolt.new went $0 → $40M ARR in ~5 months (Oct 2024–Mar 2025). Replit went ~$10M (end 2024) → $150M (Sep 2025) → $250M (Mar 2026) → ~$525M (Apr 2026). Base44 went "a few million" (Jun 2025) → $100M ARR (Mar 2026, nine months post-Wix-acquisition). These are MEASURED/dated first-party or well-sourced disclosures.
2. **Traffic and search interest peaked mid-2025 and declined even as ARR kept rising** — the classic signature of a business monetizing a shrinking-but-higher-intent cohort. Per a Barclays note reported by TechCrunch/Business Insider (Sep 26, 2025): Lovable traffic −40% from its June peak, v0 −64% since May, Bolt.new −27% since June, Replit slightly down; analysts wrote that this "begs the question on whether app/site vibecoding has peaked out already."
3. **Retention/churn is the undisclosed variable that matters most, and the disclosure that exists is ambiguous.** Lovable's Osika claims >100% net dollar retention (Nov 2025); Bolt.new CEO Eric Simons told TechCrunch (Sep 2025): "This is the problem across all these companies right now. The churn rate for everyone is really high … You have to build a retentive business." NDR >100% is compatible with heavy *logo* churn if survivors expand — so it is not evidence of durable retention.
4. **AI embedded in incumbents is the channel actually reaching ordinary SMB owners, and it is growing on MEASURED earnings-call metrics:** GoDaddy Airo annualized bookings run-rate 5× from $10M (Q1 FY2026) to $50M (Q2 FY2026); Squarespace Blueprint >50% of new customers; Wix Base44 $100M ARR + Harmony "better-than-expected" conversion.
5. **Developer sentiment is souring even as usage rises** — the defining trend-reversal signal in the professional segment. Stack Overflow 2025 (n=49,000+, fielded May 29–Jun 23, 2025): 84% use/plan to use AI (up from 76%), but trust in accuracy fell to 29% among professional developers (46% distrust; only 3.1% "highly trust"), favorability fell to 60% (from ~70% in 2024), and 66% report frustration with "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite" (the #1 frustration), with 45% losing significant time debugging AI-generated code.
6. **Standalone SMB-focused AI builders (Durable, B12, 10Web, Mixo, Butternut) appear stagnant relative to incumbents**, publishing cumulative vanity "sites built/businesses" numbers with no revenue, live-site, or cohort data, and with small/stale funding.
7. **A "vibe-coding backlash" is documented with dates** — the Tea app breach (Aug 2025), multiple Lovable security disclosures (2025–2026), the METR productivity RCT (Jul 2025), and a visible agency market that makes money *fixing* AI-generated sites.
8. **Non-adoption is mostly "no need" and "unawareness," not active rejection** (ESTIMATED split below), but active rejection is concentrated and loud among experienced developers and quality-focused agencies.

## What Can and Cannot Be Measured

**Cannot be measured directly:** the share of new websites that are "AI-generated." Platform-detection tools (W3Techs, BuiltWith, Wappalyzer) fingerprint the *platform*, not the *provenance*: a Wix site generated by Blueprint is byte-identical to a template-built Wix site. Any statistic of the form "X% of new sites are AI-built" is therefore not a measured quantity — it is an estimate or a guess. (The prior report flagged "8% of new sites in 2023 → 30%+ by 2026" and "only 29% of AI-generated sites pass Core Web Vitals" as failing sourcing; both recur in this cycle's results — e.g., a "Website Statistics 2026" aggregator page repeating the 8%/29% figures — and should still not be used.)

**Legitimate proxies, ranked by reliability (most → least):**
1. **Public-company earnings disclosures** (GoDaddy Airo bookings, Wix Base44 ARR, Squarespace Blueprint %) — MEASURED, audited context, dated. Highest reliability.
2. **Dated first-party ARR disclosures from private vendors** (Lovable, Bolt, Replit) — MEASURED but unaudited; treat run-rate as run-rate, not booked revenue.
3. **Independent traffic time-series** (Similarweb/Semrush, as aggregated by Barclays) — MEASURED-ish; confounded by bot-blocking (Vercel explicitly attributes part of v0's drop to bot mitigation).
4. **Google Trends** — MEASURED relative interest; a leading indicator of curiosity, not committed use.
5. **Repeated survey waves** (Stack Overflow, JetBrains) — MEASURED with stated methodology; best for sentiment trend.
6. **Funding rounds/cadence** — MEASURED demand signal from investors, not users.
7. **App-store/plugin installs, GitHub stars, Product Hunt/HN volume** — VENDOR-adjacent; noisy.
8. **Vendor "sites built" cumulative counts** — VENDOR-CLAIMED vanity metrics; near-worthless for trend because they never decrement and never distinguish live from abandoned.

### Implied compound growth rates (from dated points)
- **Lovable:** $200M (Nov 2025) → $500M (Jun 2026) = 2.5× in ~7 months ≈ **14% month-over-month compounding**; $100M → $500M in ~12 months = 5×.
- **Bolt.new:** $0 → $40M ARR in ~5 months — one of the fastest software ramps recorded.
- **Base44:** "a few million" → $100M ARR in nine months.
- **Replit:** ~$10M → ~$525M in ~16 months (Agent-driven, usage-metered).
- **GoDaddy Airo:** $10M → $50M annualized bookings in **one quarter** (5×), all-organic, per management.

**Interpretation:** These are genuine and extraordinary, but they are *run-rate* figures dominated by consumption/credit billing, which scales with the usage intensity of a *retained* cohort — not with net-new committed customers. That is precisely why ARR can keep rising while traffic falls.

### Is the curve steepening, flattening, or rolling over?
**Bifurcating.** Revenue and enterprise penetration are still steepening; consumer/prototyper *demand* (traffic, search interest) rolled over in mid-2025.
- **Rolling-over evidence:** Barclays traffic data (above); Google Trends peaks for "vibe coding" and named products in June–July 2025 followed by decline; Bolt.new re-architecting its subscription in Aug 2025 explicitly to fight churn; Karpathy (who coined "vibe coding" Feb 2025) later calling it "passe."
- **Still-steepening evidence:** ARR compounding through mid-2026; enterprise adoption (Lovable says roughly half of accounts are enterprise-origin; Replit cites usage across 85% of the Fortune 500); incumbent AI metrics (Airo 5× QoQ, Blueprint >50% of new customers).
- **Real plateau vs seasonal noise:** the traffic decline is too large (−40% to −64%) and too concentrated to be summer seasonality; it is a genuine demand correction in the experimental cohort — but a *correction*, not a collapse. Barclays' own conclusion was that vibe coding "isn't going away."

### Retention/churn as a trend variable
This is the most important undisclosed metric. Publicly: Lovable claims >100% NDR (Nov 2025); Bolt's CEO admits industry-wide "really high" churn. NDR >100% survives even severe logo churn if surviving users expand credit spend — so it is not evidence of durable retention. **No AI-native generator has published cohort logo-retention curves.** The only public benchmark to read them against is the SMB self-serve norm: gross dollar retention in that segment typically runs 70–88% annually. Whether later cohorts retain better (product maturing) or worse (novelty exhaustion) is **UNKNOWN** from disclosed data; the traffic reversal is consistent with novelty exhaustion in the experimental cohort while committed/enterprise cohorts deepen.

### Market forecasts (LABELED AS FORECASTS — not measured adoption)
- **Precedence Research (verbatim):** the AI-powered website builder market "accounted for USD 2.69 billion in 2025 and is predicted to increase from USD 3.24 billion in 2026 to approximately USD 17.43 billion by 2035, expanding at a CAGR of 20.55% from 2026 to 2035" (North America ~43% share, SMEs ~49% of revenue, cloud/SaaS ~81%). This is a FORECAST projected from an unstated base; not observed adoption.
- **Custom Market Insights:** AI builders were 23.6% of the total website-builder market in 2024 vs ~11% in 2022 — a vendor/analyst ESTIMATE, not a measured census.
- Other circulating forecasts (Dataintelo 20.5% CAGR to $7.9B by 2032; WiseGuy 24.66% to $29.86B by 2032) disagree with each other by ~4× on end-state size — evidence these are model outputs, not measurements. **Do not present any forecast CAGR as an observed growth rate.**

### 1. Non-technical small business owners
Adoption is real but the growth is **concentrated in incumbents' onboarding flows, not standalone AI products.** MEASURED incumbent signals: GoDaddy Airo annualized bookings 5× to $50M in one quarter (Q2 FY2026), with >70% of Airo users attaching 2+ products and improving free-to-paid conversion; Squarespace Blueprint AI now starts >50% of new customers (a TIME Best Invention of 2025); Wix Base44 hit $100M ARR and Harmony shows "better-than-expected" conversion; Hostinger says >1M users have used Horizons since launch (VENDOR-CLAIMED). By contrast, the **standalone SMB tools look stagnant**: Durable publishes cumulative "6M+ websites"/"1M+ businesses" claims (Dec 2023) that don't reconcile with later "3M+ businesses" marketing, discloses no revenue, and third-party traffic shows organic search −19% MoM (Nov 2025, Semrush); B12 last raised in 2021 ($15.7M, Tola Capital); 10Web raised only a $2M seed; Mixo (bootstrapped) and Butternut (seed, declining CB Insights Mosaic score) publish only vanity counts. **Finding: the segment is growing, and the growth sits with incumbents bundling AI into existing distribution rather than with the pure-play AI SMB startups.**

### 2. Solo founders / indie hackers / side-project builders
Plausibly the fastest-growing segment in 2024–2025 and the origin of the entire ARR spike — **confirmed, then qualified.** This cohort drove Bolt/Lovable/Replit/v0 to their record ramps. But it is also the cohort whose *traffic* rolled over first (Barclays), because its job-to-be-done is frequently *not* "get a good website" but "validate an idea," "produce a throwaway MVP," or "learn." Indie Hackers threads consistently describe the tools as "fantastic for rapid prototyping, hackathons, and validating an idea quickly" but hitting limits for "real apps that will grow" — the revealed pattern is prototype → migrate to owned code (React/Next.js/WordPress). **Trend: spike, then normalization to a smaller committed base.**

### 3. Developers and engineering teams
**Usage up, trust down — the clearest sentiment reversal in the data.** Stack Overflow 2025 (n=49,000+, fielded May 29–Jun 23, 2025): 84% use or plan to use AI tools (up from 76% in 2024); daily use 47.1%; but trust in accuracy fell to 29% among professionals (46% distrust; only 3.1% "highly trust"), favorability fell to 60% (from ~70% in 2024), 66% report frustration with "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite" (the #1 frustration), and 45% lose significant time debugging AI code. Experienced developers are most skeptical. Revealed behavior: developers use these for prototyping and boilerplate, not unreviewed production. The METR randomized controlled trial (Becker, Rush, Barnes & Rein, "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity," Jul 10, 2025, arXiv:2507.09089; 16 developers, 246 real tasks on their own mature repos) found: "Before starting tasks, developers forecast that allowing AI will reduce completion time by 24%. After completing the study, developers estimate that allowing AI reduced completion time by 20%. Surprisingly, we find that allowing AI actually increases completion time by 19%—AI tooling slowed developers down." A Fastly survey (n=791 professional developers, fielded Jul 10–14, 2025, released Aug 27, 2025) adds nuance: 28% said they fix/edit AI code often enough to offset most of the time savings, only 14% rarely need changes, and 32% of senior developers (10+ yrs) said more than half their shipped code is AI-generated. (Note: a widely-repeated "~95% of developers spend extra time fixing AI code" figure is *not* supported by the actual Fastly data and should not be used.) **Trend: adoption saturating, sentiment souring — a maturing, skeptical user base.**

### 4. Designers and agencies
**Adoption as production tooling is accelerating, but bifurcated by quality tier.** Framer is the standout — the company raised a $100M Series D at a $2B valuation (Aug 2025), surpassed Webflow in Google Trends interest in late 2025, and powers ~171k–232k live sites (BuiltWith). Agencies use Framer/Webflow AI/Relume/Locofy for speed and margin on landing-page and design-led work. But there is a large, visible countercurrent: a **rescue/cleanup market** of agencies (GetDevDone "AI Build Rescue," AssurePath "AI Project Rescue," Afterbuild Labs, WebProm) whose entire business is rebuilding AI-generated sites on WordPress/Next.js after they fail on auth, SEO, editability, and CI/CD — a strong revealed-preference signal that generated output frequently isn't production-grade. **Trend: accelerating adoption for prototyping/pitch and simple sites; resistance for complex/branded production work.**

### 5. Enterprise / marketing teams
**Brief (covered in a prior report): the newest and stickiest wedge, accelerating.** Lovable says roughly half its accounts originate from enterprises (individual → team → multimillion-dollar contract); Replit cites usage across 85% of the Fortune 500 and enterprise customers (Duolingo, Zillow) as a large revenue share; Base44 explicitly expands Wix's TAM into internal-tool/app building. The trend here is genuinely up and is the best hope for durable retention, but governance/security concerns (below) are the gating factor.

## Why People Use Them (motivation, by segment)

**Stated vs revealed reasons:**
- **SMB owners (stated):** speed ("site in 30 seconds/under a minute"), cost, no-code access, all-in-one business tools (CRM/invoicing). **Revealed:** they overwhelmingly convert *inside* an incumbent they already trust for domain/hosting — distribution, not AI novelty, drives the purchase.
- **Indie hackers (stated):** prototyping velocity, validating an idea before investing, replacing a freelancer, curiosity. **Revealed:** they pay for the *first 60–70%* (blank-screen-to-demo) and then leave or migrate — the JTBD is validation/throwaway, not a durable website.
- **Developers (stated):** speed, less boilerplate. **Revealed:** they keep AI for scaffolding/research and *retain human ownership of verification and debugging.*
- **Agencies (stated):** margin, faster delivery, more client pitches. **Revealed:** they use generators for speculative/pitch artifacts and simple sites, and bill separately to productionize.

**Job-to-be-done, made explicit:** In the indie/prototyper and (often) developer segments, the JTBD is frequently **not "get a good website"** — it is validate an idea, produce a throwaway landing page/MVP, satisfy an investor or stakeholder demo, learn to code, or generate a client pitch artifact. This is why cumulative "sites built" counts are misleading: a large fraction were never meant to live.

## Why People Don't Use Them (weighted heavily)

**Non-adoption reasons by segment (practitioner/owner accounts, not vendor commentary):**
- **Output quality / "AI slop" and generic look** — agencies describe AI sites that "look clean" but load slowly, have disconnected pages, no internal linking, no conversion tracking, no organic traffic.
- **SEO concerns** — a recurring theme in agency cleanup blogs; AI markup "was never structured for human editing" and sites launch "without internal linking, hierarchy, and intent-driven structure."
- **Editability / control** — owners "can't change a headline… without re-prompting the AI and hoping nothing else breaks."
- **Lock-in and portability** — the "I don't feel like I own my website anymore" complaint; automated AI-to-WordPress migration tools "inherit the underlying problem instead of solving it."
- **Security and code quality** — see backlash below.
- **Price** — usage/credit pricing makes heavy iteration expensive; Bolt and others raised prices, worsening churn.
- **The "last 5%" wall** — Barclays and Base44 practitioners: tools deliver the first 60–70% fast, then stall on real multi-role permissions, data isolation, integration-failure handling, and correctness under load.
- **No need / substitution** — many microbusinesses use social/marketplace profiles instead of a website.
- **Unawareness** — Barclays: "everyday consumers are still very much in the experimentation stage of AI chat interfaces… let alone… adjacent vibe coding solutions."
- **Prefer a human** — agency/freelancer/"nephew."

**Abandonment and regret (revealed-preference signal):** Indie Hackers accounts of Lovable/Bolt apps started and migrated to owned code; agency case studies of Lovable-generated sites rebuilt on WordPress/Next.js after pre-launch audits found "broken auth, disconnected forms, and no deployment pipeline." The existence of a **paid rescue industry** (GetDevDone, AssurePath, Afterbuild Labs, WebProm, plus WPConvert-style migration tools) is the strongest revealed-preference evidence that a material share of generated sites don't survive contact with production.

**The vibe-coding backlash (dated):**
- Andrej Karpathy coins "vibe coding" (Feb 2, 2025); Merriam-Webster lists it (Mar 2025); Collins names it Word of the Year 2025.
- **Lovable security exposure** — Semafor, "the hottest new vibe coding startup may be a sitting duck for hackers" (May 29, 2025); a Replit-employee scan found ~10.3% of 1,645 Lovable apps had critical vulnerabilities; further Lovable data-exposure disclosures reported into 2026.
- **Tea app breach** (Aug 2025) — an AI-assisted app exposed tens of thousands of user images/IDs and private messages via broken access-control logic; became the emblematic incident.
- **METR RCT** (Jul 2025) — 19% slower, as above.
- **Fastly survey** (Jul 2025, n=791) — 28% say fixing AI code offsets most of the time savings; only 14% rarely need changes.
- **Systemic security data** — Veracode's 2025 GenAI Code Security Report (100+ LLMs, 80 coding tasks) found that "in 45% of the cases these models introduce a detectable OWASP Top 10 security vulnerability" (avg security pass rate ~55%; Java worst at 72% failure; cross-site scripting failed in 86% of samples); Veracode's Spring 2026 re-test of newer frontier models again found flaws in 45%. IBM/GitGuardian document persistent credential exposure. (These measure *AI-generated code broadly*, not website generators specifically — MEASURED but directional.)
- **Enterprise caution** — Mendix CEO and others warning vibe coding is "deeply unreliable for enterprise use."

**Active rejection vs unawareness vs no need (ESTIMATED split, with reasoning):** No direct dataset separates these. Reasoning from Barclays' "everyday consumers are still experimenting with ChatGPT, let alone vibe coding," from the ~1-in-3 small businesses with no website at all, and from the concentration of active-rejection voices in expert (developer/agency) communities, I estimate for the total addressable population of would-be website owners: **~55–65% no need / haven't reached the trigger; ~25–35% simple unawareness; ~10–15% active rejection.** Active rejection is a *minority by headcount but a majority of the informed voice*, and it is rising among developers and quality-focused agencies. **Mark: ESTIMATED.**

## Leading Indicators and What Would Change the Trend

**Best forward-looking indicators to watch:**
1. **Incumbent earnings metrics** — GoDaddy Airo bookings run-rate and A&C bookings growth; Squarespace Blueprint conversion; Wix Base44/Harmony ARR and monetization. Highest reliability; these show whether AI reaches ordinary owners durably.
2. **Retention disclosures** — any AI-native vendor publishing *logo* retention or cohort curves (not just NDR) would be decisive. Currently absent.
3. **Traffic time-series** (Similarweb/Semrush) and **Google Trends** for named products and "vibe coding" — leading indicators of the experimental cohort; watch for a second peak or continued decline.
4. **Funding cadence** — slowing or down-rounds would signal investor recognition of the retention problem; Lovable's mid-2026 up-round talks suggest capital is still flowing.
5. **Model capability jumps** — a model that reliably closes the "last 5%" (auth, security, correctness) would re-accelerate the prototyper and SMB segments.
6. **Distribution changes** — an AI generator bundled into a registrar, POS (Square/Toast/Shopify), or OS default.

**Structural events that would accelerate the trend:** model reliability closing the production gap; a dominant distribution bundle (registrar/POS/OS); enterprise governance tooling that makes generated code auditable; convergence of visual editing + vibe coding (Wix's stated thesis).

**Structural events that would kill or stall it:** a catastrophic, widely-publicized breach traceable to a named generator; employer/enterprise bans; regulatory liability for insecure generated code; sustained unit-economics failure (inference costs > credit revenue — a real risk given per-inference model costs) forcing price hikes that accelerate churn; or the incumbents simply absorbing the entire SMB opportunity, leaving standalones without a market.

## What the Evidence Does and Doesn't Support

**Established (MEASURED or well-sourced):**
- AI-native generators posted among the fastest ARR ramps in software history (2024–2026), dated above.
- Traffic/interest for leading standalone tools peaked mid-2025 and declined materially (Barclays).
- Developer usage rose while trust fell (Stack Overflow repeated waves).
- AI embedded in incumbents is growing on audited earnings metrics and is the channel reaching ordinary SMB owners.
- A dated series of security incidents and a paid rescue industry exist.

**Open / unverified (UNKNOWN):**
- Logo-level retention and cohort survival for any AI-native generator.
- The true share of new *live* websites that are AI-generated (unmeasurable by platform detection).
- Whether later cohorts retain better or worse than early ones.
- Standalone SMB tools' actual revenue and live-site survival.
- Whether the mid-2025 traffic decline is a one-off correction or the start of a longer plateau.

- **"Only 29% of AI-generated sites pass Core Web Vitals"** — recurs on the same aggregator pages; no reproducible methodology. **Do not use.**

## Caveats
- Most private-vendor ARR figures are self-reported run-rates, unaudited; treat "ARR" as annualized run-rate, not booked revenue.
- Consumption/credit pricing inflates ARR growth relative to committed-customer growth — the two must not be conflated.
- Traffic-tool data is confounded by bot-blocking (Vercel's v0 case).
- Security studies (Veracode, GitGuardian, METR) measure AI-generated *code* broadly, not website generators specifically; the read-across is directional.
- The METR RCT has a small sample (16 developers) and measures *experienced* developers on *their own mature repos* — its "19% slower" result should not be over-generalized to novices or greenfield projects.
- The active-rejection/unawareness/no-need split is ESTIMATED, not measured.
