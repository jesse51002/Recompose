# AI-customized white-label apps: market and competitive map

**Bottom line: the thesis sits in genuine whitespace, but in a smaller pure-coaching market and a much larger but more crowded creator market.** No platform today combines AI-generated per-customer design systems (illustrations, iconography, typography, motion, copy) + fixed vertical structure + native iOS/Android + SaaS pricing. The closest competitors — Passion.io, Mighty Pro, Disciple, Uscreen, Trainerize, Nudge Coach — all deliver white-label apps but rely on **template + logo/color theming** with human-delivered design priced at $200–$1,500+/mo, validating both the demand and the pricing arbitrage AI enables. The creator vertical offers a 50M–200M+ TAM and bigger revenue per customer; the coaching vertical is smaller (~$5.3B globally per ICF, ~167K coaches by 2025) but has lower platform saturation and clearer fixed-structure norms. **Fitness/health coaching is the largest sub-segment by headcount** (~128K US health coaches, ~17M+ US personal-training users), and Trainerize already proved 400K+ trainers will pay for branded apps — making fitness the most de-risked entry vertical.

## Part 1 — Market sizing

### Creator economy: $250B today, $480B by 2027, but most spending is not on tools

Goldman Sachs anchors the creator economy at **$250B in 2023, projected to ~$480B by 2027** at a 10–20% CAGR. Other estimates run wider: SNS Insider puts 2024 at $203.6B, Statista/Collabstr at $191.5B in 2025 → $528B by 2030 (22.5% CAGR). The **US creator economy is roughly $51B in 2024** and ~37% of the global market (Market.us, SNS Insider). The narrower **influencer marketing slice grew from $17.4B (2023) to $32.55B (2025)**, projected $40B+ by 2026 (Influencer Marketing Hub Benchmark Report 2025; Aspire 2025). 82% of global influencer marketing spend flows to US creators (Sociallyin 2026).

**Active creator counts vary 4x by definition.** Goldman/SignalFire's foundational figure is **50M global creators**; Linktree, Whop, and Statista cite **~200M+**; Market.us claims 400M+ by 2024. SignalFire's bottom-up framework — still the most rigorous — splits this into ~2M professional creators (1M YouTube channels with >10K subs, 500K Instagram accounts >100K followers, 300K Twitch Partners/Affiliates) and ~46M amateur/semi-pro creators. **Only ~4% of global creators earn over $100K/year** (Goldman 2023). ConvertKit/Kit's 2024 State of the Creator Economy survey (n=1,004) found **18% earned over $100K** in their sample, **28% are self-employed full-time creators**, and 65% are female.

### Tier breakdown (Instagram, Influencer Marketing Hub 2025)

The pyramid is bottom-heavy: **nano-influencers (1K–10K) make up 75.9% of Instagram's influencer base**, and ~9 in 10 Instagram influencers have under 50K followers. Linktree's median creator has just 4,000 followers (Linktree Creator Report). Engagement inverts the size curve — nano creators see 5.6–8%+ engagement vs. 0.6–1.2% for mega creators (1M+) — making the long tail commercially valuable to brands and a meaningful customer base for SaaS tools.

### Creator software spending: small per-creator, big aggregate

Adobe data cited by Whop in 2026 puts **average creator tech expenses at $1,000–$2,000/year**, with $10,700 average seed money to launch. Outfame's 2026 data shows creators investing **$49–$129/month in growth tools** (~$600–$1,500/yr), or ~12% of revenue. **Implied SaaS TAM**: 50–200M creators × 10–20% paid penetration × ~$1,000/year ≈ **$5–40B+ creator software TAM**. The number of creator-economy platforms exploded from 1,120 (2019) to 6,939 (2025), a 520% increase (Influencer Marketing Hub via Archive.com). Creator-economy startups raised **>$767M globally across 2023–2024**, with AI startups capturing the largest share (Whop).

### Coaching: smaller, fragmented, doubling fast

The pure-coaching market is far smaller than headlines suggest. **ICF's 2025 Global Coaching Study (PwC) reports $5.34B in global coach-practitioner revenue** with **122,974 active coach practitioners worldwide** — up 15% from 2023 and roughly double 2019's 71,000. North America accounts for 31% of coaches at $2.09B revenue, with Western Europe at 28%. The average coach earns **$52,800/year globally, $67,800 in North America**, charging $244/hour globally and $272 in North America — though **53% of coaches earn under $30K/year from coaching**, exposing a long tail of price-sensitive solopreneurs.

US-specific figures vary by definition. **Marketdata LLC (Dec 2025) estimates 232,000+ US coaches across a $16B industry** (life + health + executive combined), more than doubled since 2016. **IBISWorld pegs US "Business Coaching" (NAICS 61143) at $20B in 2025** across 72,013 businesses, but this includes management/professional development training. Pure life coaching is ~$2.1B (IBISWorld 2024). Both are highly fragmented — no single firm has more than 5% share.

By specialty, ICF 2023 finds **67% of coaches identify as business/leadership/executive coaches**, with leadership the single largest sub-specialty at 34%. Health and wellness coaching is a separate, faster-growing market: **$20.1B globally in 2025** at 8.3% CAGR (Dimension Market Research), with **~128K US health coaches** (Research and Markets 2025). The fitness/personal trainer segment dwarfs everything by headcount: HFA's 2025 data shows **77M US fitness facility members and ~17M people using personal trainers in 2024** across 114,370 US clubs, with **median PT pay of $46,953/year**.

### Coach software spending: a real data gap

**No published industry survey reports average coach software spend** — this is the single largest research gap. Bottom-up from product pricing, a typical solo coach stack (CoachAccountable $40, Calendly $12, Zoom $15, Kajabi $179, etc.) totals **$100–$500/month or $1,200–$6,000/year**. The proxy "online coaching software market" is **$2.26B (2023) projected to $8.16B by 2032** at 15.3% CAGR (Global Growth Insights). ICF 2025 reports just **19% of coaches invested in new technology last year**, expected to rise to 27% within three years — a slow-but-rising adoption signal that argues for a small first-mover window.

## Part 2 — Competitive map

The competitive landscape splits into a clean dichotomy: **cheap web platforms with template-only customization OR expensive ($700–$1,500+/mo) white-label app builders with human-delivered design**. The thesis sits in the unfilled middle. Below, every platform is rated on whether it overlaps with the thesis (AI-generated per-customer design + fixed vertical structure + native app + SaaS pricing).

### All-in-one creator/coach platforms

The category leaders dominate distribution but not design. **Kajabi (~100K creators, $10B in lifetime creator earnings, $2B+ valuation)** raised prices in late 2025 ($89–$499/mo, with branded mobile apps as a $199/mo add-on or included on Pro). Its September 2025 "Kajabi Evolved" relaunch added AI for content (course outlines, email copy, 100-language video dubbing, transcripts) but **no AI design generation** — verdict: **partially overlapping** because of the branded app, fully missing the AI-design layer. **Teachable, Thinkific ($199/mo branded app add-on), and Podia** are template LMSs with no AI design and either no native app or a generic shared one — verdict: **no overlap**.

**Linktree (50M+ users, ~80% link-in-bio market share, $1B+ valuation)** and **Beacons.ai (3M+ creators)** are link-in-bio tools, not app builders. Beacons does generate AI marketing assets (product images, outreach emails, media kits) — verdict: **partially**, but for marketing collateral, not app design. **Stan Store** ($29–$99/mo, 100K+ creators, $390M+ paid out) and **Gumroad** are mobile-web storefronts without branded apps. **Patreon (286K paid creators, $10B lifetime payouts)**, **Substack (50M subscribers, 5M+ paid, 17K paid writers)**, and **Passes ($65M+ raised, ~1,000 elite creators)** all use their own branded apps with minimal creator customization — verdict: **no overlap**.

**Skool** is the philosophical opposite of the thesis: confirmed "intentionally limited customization" — no color customization, font updates, or dark mode, with users actively requesting branding controls in Skool's own forum and being declined. **Mighty Networks/Mighty Pro** is the closest direct competitor in this category. The Mighty Pro tier delivers white-label native iOS/Android apps in 3–4 weeks via a dedicated Community Strategist (used by Tony Robbins, Marie Forleo, Jim Kwik, Matthew Hussey). Its "Mighty Co-Host" AI generates initial logos, brand imagery, and community templates at signup — closer to the thesis than anyone else, but **the actual design is still human-delivered**, priced at custom enterprise rates that explicitly market "instead of $50K–$200K custom dev." Verdict: **partially overlapping; closest direct threat in the community-app segment**. **Circle.so** offers similar white-label apps via "Circle Plus" custom-priced tier alongside operational AI (moderation, copilot). Verdict: **partially**.

### Coach-specific platforms

These are coaching CRMs with fixed vertical structure but minimal design customization. **CoachAccountable** ($20–$400/mo by client count), **Practice.do** ($5–$50/mo, 99% solo business owners), **Paperbell** ($57/mo flat, ~14K customers serving 100K+ clients), **Satori, Quenza, Delenta, Simply.Coach** (all $9–$87/mo) and **CoachVantage** offer client portals with logo/color branding only. None have native white-label apps or AI design. Verdict: **no overlap** for all.

**Nudge Coach is the closest fit in the coach vertical** — it specifically markets a "white-labeled client mobile app launched in under a week" using pre-made templates, targeting "coaches, creators, and influencers" at SaaS pricing. Customization is template + logo, not AI-generated. Verdict: **partially — strongest indicator that demand for the thesis exists in coaching**. In fitness, **TrueCoach (16K coaches), Trainerize (400K+ trainers, 45K+ businesses, white-label app on Studio tier $250+/mo)**, and **Everfit (white-label on Enterprise; AI for workout generation only)** prove the model works. Trainerize especially **validates that 400K+ trainers will pay for branded apps with fixed structure and theme-level customization**.

### Website builders, app builders, and AI design tools

**Squarespace's Blueprint AI** (TIME Best Inventions 2025, free across $16–$49/mo plans, ~5.2M subscribers, taken private at $7.2B) is the closest mainstream execution of "AI generates a personalized site." Squarespace reports **>50% of new customers now start with Blueprint**, which generates copy, curates imagery, and recommends color/font/section combinations. But output remains curated-template based and Squarespace has no native mobile app product. Verdict: **partially — the proof point that AI-generated personalized design works at consumer scale**. **Wix's Harmony AI** (250M+ registered users) and **Wixel** generate sites and design assets but not native apps. **Webflow, Bubble (3M+ apps), Glide, Softr, Carrd** are horizontal builder tools, not vertical SaaS — verdict: **no overlap** for the thesis specifically.

White-label app builders prove the cost arbitrage but lack AI design. **GoodBarber** (30K+ apps, reseller from $215/mo), **BuildFire** (10K+ apps, $165–$440/mo), **Bravo Studio** ($22–$237/mo), **Adalo** (3M+ apps, "Magic Start" generates app foundations from text), **Draftbit, AppMySite** all offer white-label native apps with theme-based customization — none generate bespoke design systems. Adalo's Magic Start is the closest to the thesis among horizontal app builders but produces template-driven UIs, not full per-customer design. Verdict: **partially** for the white-label/reseller players; **no overlap** on AI design generation.

The pure AI design tools are the closest technical analogs but aren't productized for verticals. **Galileo AI was acquired by Google in May 2025 and rebranded as Stitch** (free, Gemini 2.5–powered) — generates multi-screen UI mockups with realistic content, the highest-fidelity AI UI generation available, but produces screens not full design systems and is a designer tool, not deployable product. **Uizard's Autodesigner 2.0** (acquired by Miro May 2024, $12–$39/mo) generates editable multi-screen prototypes plus theme generation. **Framer AI's Wireframer** generates web layouts from prompts. **Lovable.dev** ($20M ARR in 2 months) and **Bolt.new** ($40M ARR in 6 months) generate full-stack web apps from prompts but produce generic-looking design and don't build native iOS/Android. **Durable.co** ($15–$95/mo) generates business websites in 30 seconds. **v0 by Vercel** (6M+ developers) generates React/Tailwind components. Verdict across the AI tooling category: **partially overlapping on the technology, no overlap on the productized vertical SaaS** — these tools could be ingredients in the thesis stack, not competitors to it.

### The tightest direct competitors: white-label app makers for creators

This is where the thesis's strongest direct competition sits.

| Platform | Pricing | Users | AI design? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Passion.io** | $99–$599/mo SaaS; $10K–$20K Plus DFY | Tens of thousands of coaches/creators | "AI Coach" for content only | **Highest direct overlap** — same ICP, same pricing, same fixed-structure premise; missing only AI design |
| **Mighty Pro** | Custom (~enterprise SaaS) | Hundreds of premium hosts (Tony Robbins, Marie Forleo, etc.) | Mighty Co-Host generates logo/brand at signup | **High overlap** — closest on AI brand-generation, but design human-delivered |
| **Disciple Media** | $308–$1,167+/mo | 700+ communities, 2M+ end-members | None | **High overlap** — pure white-label app pure-play, template-driven design |
| **Uscreen** | $149–$499+/mo | 25K+ businesses, 9.7M end-users | None | **High overlap** — video-vertical white-label apps with templated design |
| **Tapcart** | $250–$1,000+/mo | $5.5B GMV across Shopify | TapAI for push, recommendations | **Partial** — proves the playbook but eCommerce-only |
| **VidApp, Maestro** | Tiered SaaS / $99+/mo | Smaller | None | **Partial** — adjacent video-creator markets |

**Passion.io is the most direct competitor**: identical target ICP (coaches/creators), identical SaaS pricing tier, identical fixed-structure premise, with branding limited to logo/color/template selection. The thesis differentiates almost entirely by adding AI-generated bespoke design (illustrations, iconography, typography, motion, copy tone). **Mighty Pro's Mighty Co-Host AI is the single closest existing AI-design feature** in the white-label app space, but it generates a logo and onboarding template, not a deep design system, and the actual app is still human-styled.

### Custom dev agencies: the alternative being disrupted

Custom native app development costs **$50K–$250K upfront with 15–25% annual maintenance** (cited by Passion.io, Mighty Pro, multiple agencies). This is the labor-intensive incumbent that the thesis directly disrupts: AI compresses styling labor, collapsing this from a six-figure project into a $200–$1,000/month SaaS subscription.

### Supporting stack tools (not competitors)

**Kit/ConvertKit** (600K+ creators, $39–$619/mo by list size), **Calendly** (20M+ users, $12–$20/seat/mo), **Acuity Scheduling** ($20–$61/mo), **Mailchimp** (14M+ users), and **HoneyBook** (100K+ small businesses) are stack components creators and coaches use alongside any branded app — complementary, not competitive.

## Part 3 — Tool adoption percentages

### What creators actually use

The link-in-bio category is concentrated and well-measured. **Linktree commands roughly 80% market share among link-in-bio users** with 50M+ users (BuiltWith, TechCrunch May 2024), processing $6B in annual GMV and 1.3B monthly clicks. **Beacons reaches ~2–3M creators**, while Milkshake (3.7%), Linkin.bio (1.6%), and Bio.site (1.3%) split the rest. **Stan Store** sits separately as a paid storefront with ~100K creators having earned $390M+. Email is dominant in the creator stack: Kit/ConvertKit's 600K+ creators sent **28.46 billion emails in 2023** at 902 emails/sec, and **27% of creators name email as their best engagement channel vs. 15% for Instagram and just 2% for TikTok** (ConvertKit 2024 survey).

Subscription/membership platforms have hard adoption numbers. **Patreon: 286K+ paid creators, 10M+ paying members, $10B+ lifetime payouts** (2024 valuation $1.5B, down from $4B 2021 peak). **Substack: 17K+ paid writers, 5M+ paid subscriptions** (up from 1M in 2021), $1.1B valuation after July 2025 Series C. **Kajabi: 100K+ creators, $10B in lifetime creator earnings**, with average Kajabi creator earning $190K/year — heavily skewed toward coaches and course-led businesses. AI tool adoption among creators is now near-universal: **84% use AI in 2026, 91% use generative AI to scale content output, 63% use AI scriptwriting tools** (DemandSage, Vocal Media, Whop 2024 data).

Content-format mix from ConvertKit/Kit 2024: **58% produce email/newsletters, 51% articles/books, 30% educational courses, 23% short-form video** (down from 45% in 2022) — note the surprising contraction in short-form video and surge of email and courses, both directly relevant to the thesis verticals.

### What coaches actually use

ICF 2025's primary findings: **47% of coaches use digital coaching platforms**, but only **19% invested in new technology in the past year** (rising to an expected 27% within three years). This is the single most important adoption signal — coach platform penetration is low and rising, with significant whitespace.

Secondary aggregator data (WifiTalents) — directional only — reports:
- 83% of coaching sessions delivered via Zoom or Teams
- 68% of coaches use automated scheduling such as Calendly
- 89% use LinkedIn for B2B networking; 56% use social media as primary lead gen
- 15% use AI for note-taking/summarization (early-stage adoption)

Coach-specific platform user counts remain modest against the 122K–167K coach TAM. **Paperbell reports 14K+ customers serving 100K+ end-clients**; CoachAccountable cites "thousands of coaches"; Practice, Simply.Coach, Satori, Delenta each appear to be in the low thousands. Combined, coach-specific SaaS likely covers under 50K of an addressable 167K coaches — **a meaningful 60–70% whitespace**. **Kajabi (~100K creators, ~58% US) is probably the single most-used platform by coaches**, despite being a generic knowledge-commerce tool. Enterprise coaching is a separate world: **BetterUp's network of 4,000+ coaches** sells multi-year contracts at $850K–$4.5M annually; **CoachHub** has 3,500+ coaches in 90 countries serving 60+ enterprises with hybrid AI coaching; **Torch** has 2,000+ coaches.

Mobile is winning on the client side: **54.6% of coaching platform access is now via mobile apps** (Future Market Insights 2026). Among fitness coaches' clients, **22.6% of US fitness facility members worked with a personal trainer in 2024** — a record high — and Trainerize alone serves 400K+ trainers and 45K+ fitness businesses with branded apps, giving the clearest proof point that the white-label app model has product-market fit at scale.

## Synthesis and strategic implications

**The thesis whitespace is real and concrete**: no platform combines AI-generated per-customer design + fixed vertical structure + native app + SaaS pricing. The cost arbitrage is also real — Mighty Pro, Disciple, Passion.io DFY, and custom agencies all charge $10K+ setup or $700+/month *because* design is delivered by humans. AI compressing styling labor closes a genuine gap, not a narrative one.

The two verticals trade off cleanly. **Creators are the larger market** ($250B economy, 50M–200M global creators, established white-label app demand at premium prices, mature stack categories) but **face Passion.io, Mighty Pro, and Disciple as direct competitors**, plus the latent threat of Kajabi adding design AI given its $2B war chest and September 2025 AI relaunch. **Coaches are smaller** ($5.3B pure-coaching, 122K coaches globally) but **far less platform-saturated** — only 47% use digital platforms at all, only 19% invested in new tech last year, and the strongest coach-specific player (Paperbell, ~14K customers) has barely scratched the addressable base. The coach vertical has clearer fixed-structure norms (sessions + packages + payments + client portal) and weaker incumbents.

**Fitness/health coaching is the highest-leverage entry point**: 128K US health coaches, 17M+ US personal-training users, Trainerize's 400K+ trainers proving willingness-to-pay for branded apps, and **Everfit's AI is workout-only — design is wide open**. The vertical is also underserved by AI design tools because the major builders (Squarespace, Galileo/Stitch, Lovable, Bolt) are horizontal-web focused.

The most underestimated competitive risk is not Passion.io or Mighty Pro themselves but **Kajabi, Squarespace, or Wix** adding AI design generation to their existing distribution. Squarespace's >50% Blueprint AI adoption among new sign-ups is the proof that a horizontal incumbent can move into AI-generated personalized design successfully. **The thesis's defensibility depends on going deeper than these horizontal players will: bespoke design systems with motion, iconography, illustration, and copy tone tuned to a specific vertical** — outputs they have no incentive to build because their customers span hundreds of verticals.

**Three numbers worth grounding decisions in:** $5.34B total global coach revenue (ICF 2025) vs. $250B creator economy (Goldman 2023) sets the TAM ratio at ~47:1 in favor of creators. But coach platform penetration sits below 50% vs. near-universal tooling among monetizing creators. And fitness coaching alone — 128K US health coaches plus ~17M PT users — is large enough to build a $100M+ ARR business inside a single coaching sub-vertical, while creator-vertical entrants face four direct competitors with thousands to millions of users already. The asymmetry argues for entering **fitness/health coaching first**, proving the AI-design + vertical-structure model, then expanding into business/life coaching, then entering creators with the muscle that's been built.
