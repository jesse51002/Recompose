# The Landscape of Embedded / White-Label Website-Builder Suppliers

## TL;DR
- The embedded/white-label website-builder supply market is real, established (~15+ years old), and clearly bifurcated: a **hosting/telco/bank OEM tier** dominated by proprietary, deeply-integrated suppliers (BaseKit, Site.pro, Mono Solutions, Duda, WebPros/Sitejet, IONOS/CM4all) and an **agency/SaaS reseller tier** of lighter-touch, self-serve "white-label dashboard" vendors (10Web, Vendasta, Simvoly, Weblium, Ucraft, SiteSwan, DashClicks, GoHighLevel). Several seed-list names — Framer, Squarespace, Universe, Strikingly, Unstack, Durable, B12, Bookmark, and Brizy-as-primary — do NOT belong as genuine OEM suppliers and are flagged explicitly below.
- AI site generation is now near-universal as a *feature* but shallow in *kind*: almost every supplier does content-generation-into-templates or section-assembly; **genuine layout/structure synthesis is claimed by few and independently verified by none**. The category's real 2024–2026 shift is data-fed generation (pulling Google Business Profile / partner catalog data into the generated site), not structural synthesis.
- Partner/OEM pricing is almost universally undisclosed (BaseKit, Mono, Duda-OEM, Yola all bespoke — a genuine negative result); the rare exceptions with published partner rates are Site.pro ($140/mo for 500 sites, ≈$0.28/site/mo), SiteSwan ($149–$300/mo), and Simvoly ($59–$129/mo). Output portability is poor across the board — proprietary hosted runtimes dominate; 10Web (WordPress output) is the notable portable exception.

## Key Findings

1. **The category splits cleanly by buyer type.** Hosting providers, telcos, and banks buy from a small set of suppliers built for deep integration, on-premise/data-residency options, and control-panel distribution (BaseKit, Site.pro, Mono, WebPros/Sitejet, Duda enterprise, IONOS/CM4all). Agencies and light SaaS buy self-serve reseller dashboards (10Web, Vendasta, Simvoly, Weblium, Ucraft, SiteSwan, DashClicks, GoHighLevel). Different suppliers dominate each segment; overlap is limited.

2. **Control-panel distribution (cPanel, Plesk, WHMCS, DirectAdmin) is a major, under-appreciated channel.** WebPros — owner of cPanel and Plesk — shipped its own Sitejet Builder natively into cPanel and Plesk, and per its release rolled out a free AI Website Generator on **August 19, 2025** at no additional cost, which "leverages AI to help users instantly create complete and flawlessly responsive websites, including layout, structure, and written content." This instantly distributed a builder (with structure-level generation claims) across a vast installed base of hosting providers. Site.pro integrates as a module into ~12 different control panels. This is a structurally different — and defensible — distribution model than API-embed.

3. **Several seed-list names are NOT embedded/white-label suppliers and should be reclassified.** Framer, Squarespace, Universe, Strikingly, Unstack, Durable, and B12 are direct-to-end-user (and in some cases agency) builders with no genuine OEM/embed program — B12 explicitly has "no API availability," and Durable is a solopreneur-facing product. Wix and Elementor offer partner/affiliate programs but not true invisible-infrastructure embedding. Bookmark shut down (2023–2024). Weebly was absorbed into Square. Mobirise is an offline desktop app. These are documented below rather than dropped silently.

4. **AI generation has landed as a feature but not as a capability leap.** By 2026 nearly every supplier advertises "AI website generation." The evidence shows most are doing (i) content generation into fixed templates or (ii) section assembly. 10Web ("agentic" WordPress generation) and Site.pro (conversational AI editor) and WebPros/Sitejet (structure-level claims) make the strongest claims. **No supplier has independently-verified genuine layout/structure synthesis producing designs outside a fixed section library.** The real observable frontier is **data-fed generation** — Mono's Quick Creator AI and GoDaddy Airo pull Google Business Profile data; this is field-population, not structural decision-making.

5. **Ownership is consolidating under strategics and PE.** Mono Solutions passed from independent → Bauer Media Group (2019) → (reportedly Ten Oaks Group, 2022) → a three-publisher European consortium in January 2025. Sitejet → WebPros (2021). Weebly → Square (2018). Zyro folded into Hostinger. WE22/CM4all → IONOS. The independent, venture-backed pure-play (Duda, 10Web, B12) is now the minority structure.

6. **Partner pricing and tenant-publish rates are the two biggest evidence gaps.** Most OEM pricing is bespoke and unpublished — a genuine negative result, not an oversight. And no supplier publishes what fraction of a partner's tenants actually publish and maintain a live site; this is UNKNOWN across the entire set.

## Details by Dimension

### Company / ownership / funding

**Duda** (Israel/US) — Founded 2009 by Itai Sadan and Amir Glatt; HQ Palo Alto with a Tel Aviv office. Raised $100M total; last round the $50M Series D of June 15, 2021 (Claridge IL, Susquehanna Growth Equity, Vintage Investment Partners). Independent. Per prior work in this thread: ~$41.2M ARR estimate, ~22,000 orgs / ~1M sites claim, Core Web Vitals leadership, no revenue share on partner widgets, static-export limitations, "Populate Template with AI" API feature (Jan 28 2026), no funding since June 2021. Included here as one comparability row; not re-litigated.

**BaseKit** (UK) — Founded 2008, HQ Bristol. Investors include Nauta Capital, NESTA Investments, Eden Ventures, Seedcamp. B Corp certified. The most explicitly telco/bank/hosting-focused OEM supplier; markets to "hosting providers, challenger banks, telcos, and SaaS companies." Claims 60+ partners across 40 markets (some pages say "100+" — an internal inconsistency). Named partners: Telefónica, Fasthosts, Etisalat, Miss Group, Sri Lanka Telecom/SLTMobitel, Acens, Yourhosting; Mastercard partnership for MSME digital inclusion.

**Site.pro** (Lithuania) — Founder Filip Borcov (Borcov Group). 100% white-label builder purpose-built for hosting providers and domain registrars. One of very few suppliers publishing partner pricing.

**Mono Solutions** (Denmark) — Founded 2007, Copenhagen, by Louise Lachmann (CEO/co-founder) and David W. Højelsen (CTO). Acquired by Bauer Media Group July 18, 2019 (served "70+ digital service providers" then); reportedly transferred to a Ten Oaks Group affiliate in 2022 (aggregator-sourced, not a Mono primary release — flagged). Per Mono's own release dated January 9, 2025, "the deal was closed on January 9, 2025" with three European publishers — **FCR Media (Belgium), 1881 Group AS (Norway), and heise (Germany)** — holding **equal shares**; CEO named is **Jeppe Rosfeldt**, and combined the three publishers "support 140,000 business clients and 500,000 private customers with a total revenue of €330 Mio." Positions itself as "the European alternative" to US suppliers. Claims 70+ reseller partners and 250k+ subscriptions across 30+ markets (one page says "50+" — flagged). Headcount reported 30 (Crunchbase/PitchBook) to 51–200 (ZoomInfo) — conflicting, no primary figure. Standalone revenue not published.

**10Web** (Armenia/US) — AI-powered, WordPress-based white-label builder. VP of product Suren Shahnazaryan. Claims 2M+ website generations across 1,000+ B2B partners; "one of the world's top 10 hosting companies runs 10Web's AI builder under their own brand" (unnamed — vendor claim). Three integration paths: white-label API, self-hosted WordPress plugin (via cPanel/Plesk WordPress Toolkit), and reseller dashboard.

**Vendasta** (Canada) — Agency-enablement platform; Website Pro component is WordPress-based on Google Cloud. Claims 66,000+ partners. Also embeds its broader "AI Workforce" via REST APIs with SOC 2 compliance. Primary motion is agency/reseller enablement, but it offers embed via API — qualifies, with embed as one of several motions.

**B12** (US) — Founded 2015 by Nitesh Banta and Adam Marcus; HQ New York; raised $28.1M (Series B; investors incl. General Catalyst, Breyer Capital, SV Angel, Tola Capital, Naval Ravikant). AI + human-expert builder for professional-services firms. **No public API / OEM embed program found ("no API availability is mentioned") — B12 is a direct-to-SMB builder and does NOT belong as an embedded supplier.**

**Durable** (Canada) — Vancouver; per its own release and TechCrunch, "$14 Million in Series A funding" (December 13, 2023) led by Spark Capital with Torch Capital, Altman Capital, Dash Fund, South Park Commons, Infinity Ventures, and Soma Capital, "with over $20 Million in total funds raised to date"; founder/CEO James Clift; "over six million websites" built in the first year (now claims 10M+). **Direct-to-solopreneur; no OEM/embed program found — does NOT belong.**

**WebPros / Sitejet** — Sitejet founded 2018, acquired by WebPros (owner of cPanel, Plesk, WHMCS) in 2021. Sitejet Builder now ships natively inside cPanel and Plesk; free AI Website Generator rolled out August 19, 2025. Two products: Sitejet Studio (agencies) and Sitejet Builder (hosting-panel end users).

**GoDaddy** (US, public, NYSE: GDDY) — Airo Site Designer (global rollout 2025) generates WordPress sites from a plain-text prompt. Builds/owns tech in-house. Primarily direct-to-SMB; not an OEM supplier to other platforms.

**IONOS / 1&1** (Germany) — Builder built on acquired proprietary tech: IONOS acquired WE22 AG (maker of white-label builder CM4all, 5M+ sites) for an undisclosed sum. MyWebsite Now (2023) with AI Website Generator launched September 12, 2023. CM4all historically served other hosts white-label; whether IONOS actively OEMs today is unconfirmed.

**Yola** (South Africa → San Francisco) — Founded March 2007 as Synthasite in Cape Town by Vinny Lingham and Brent Viljoen; renamed Yola 2009. ~76 employees; current CEO Trevor Harries-Jones. Runs a dual B2C + white-label model; named enterprise partners not disclosed. AI builder present.

**SiteSwan** (US) — 100% white-label builder for agencies/designers/newspapers building SMB sites; ~1,800 resellers claimed. Publishes partner pricing (rare). Explicitly does not sell direct to SMBs (no channel conflict). Output explicitly non-portable.

**Weblium / Ucraft** (Ukraine / Armenia) — Both offer white-label reseller and API/embed tiers. Weblium 300+ templates; Ucraft offers enterprise self-hosting on partner Google Kubernetes instances.

**Simvoly, DashClicks, GoHighLevel** (US) — All-in-one agency/SaaS platforms with white-label website+funnel builders and reseller/"SaaS mode" billing. GoHighLevel's builder is embedded within a CRM-first platform; SaaS mode lets agencies rebrand and resell.

**Bookmark** — AI builder (AiDA), founded 2015, shut down 2023–2024 after failing to raise; no acquirer of the AI tech recorded.

**Brizy** — Brizy Cloud offers white-label plans (Agency $199/mo with API; Enterprise with on-prem/SSO). Primary motion is agency/SaaS reseller; API/SSO embed is available. Qualifies as a secondary-embed player.

### Embed mechanism

- **REST API:** Duda (mature Partner API, webhooks, MCP server), 10Web (Gateway API), BaseKit (REST + on-premise), Site.pro (custom API), Vendasta (REST, SOC 2), Brizy (API on Agency+ tiers), Weblium (API-based SaaS embed), Mono (API integrations; dedicated Quick Creator API announced late-2024 as forthcoming — not confirmed shipped).
- **Control-panel module:** Site.pro (cPanel, Plesk, DirectAdmin, ISPmanager, Webuzo, InterWorx, CWP, Hosting Controller, Vesta, Hestia, LiveConfig, ZPanel), WebPros/Sitejet (native in cPanel/Plesk), 10Web (cPanel/Plesk WordPress Toolkit plugin), BaseKit (cPanel, Plesk, WHMCS, Parallels APS).
- **On-premise / self-hosted:** BaseKit (SaaS or on-premise), Site.pro (Cloud or On-Premises, data on partner servers), Ucraft (Enterprise on partner GKE), IM Creator ("Host it yourself"). These are the strongest fits for regulated bank/telco buyers needing data residency.
- **Reseller dashboard (lighter embed):** 10Web, Vendasta, Simvoly, Weblium, SiteSwan, DashClicks, GoHighLevel, Yola.

### White-label depth and system of record

BaseKit and Mono make the partner the system of record: end customers are the partner's, and BaseKit's privacy policy explicitly disclaims the reseller's customers ("If you are a customer of another company that offers BaseKit's website builder, please see the company's privacy policy"). Site.pro leaves "no trace" — invoices "just mention a purchase of a website builder." IM Creator sends emails from a generic domain with the partner's brand name. Duda's white-label preview shows the agency's brand, not Duda's. In the deep-OEM tier the supplier is invisible and never contacts end customers; in the reseller-dashboard tier the same is generally true but billing/comms configurability varies.

### Output and portability

- **WordPress output (portable):** 10Web, Vendasta Website Pro, GoDaddy Airo, and WebPros/Sitejet (WordPress support signposted). 10Web is the standout portable option — output is standard WordPress with the plugin/theme ecosystem.
- **Proprietary hosted runtime (non-portable):** Duda, BaseKit, Mono, Simvoly, Yola, SiteSwan, Ucraft, Weblium, Strikingly, Universe. SiteSwan states explicitly it is proprietary and sites cannot be hosted elsewhere; a uKit review confirms it "will not let you move your web pages to a different hosting service."
- **HTML/PHP static-ish:** Site.pro outputs HTML + PHP with no database on Nginx — more technically portable than DB-driven builders but still tied to their runtime.
- Duda's static-export limitation was documented in prior work. **No documented case of a platform fully migrating OFF a supplier, and what it took, was found — a negative result.**

### Generation — classification against evidence

- **Content generation into fixed templates (i):** GoDaddy Airo (NLP generates headlines/descriptions/CTAs into industry templates per hands-on reviews), B12, Durable, Mono Quick Creator AI, IONOS AI generator, Yola, most reseller-dashboard vendors. Evidence: product docs describe answering ~3 questions → populated template.
- **Section assembly (ii):** Sitejet AI Website Generator (generates a page with content and layout, editable in drag-and-drop; WebPros claims "layout, structure, and written content"), Wix ADI-lineage tools.
- **Fuller generation claims (toward iii, unverified):** 10Web claims "agentic" generation producing full page structure, copy, and layout as production-ready WordPress ("team of AI agents"); Site.pro offers a conversational/agentic AI editor that moves elements and adds sections via chat. These are the strongest claims but rest on vendor marketing; **no independent verification of genuine layout/structure synthesis (designs not drawn from a fixed section library) exists for any supplier.**
- Template libraries where disclosed: Site.pro 200+, Weblium 300+, Sitejet 170+, SiteSwan 300+; Ucraft/others unstated.

### Data inputs from the partner platform (first-class dimension)

- **Brand/identity only (a):** the default for nearly all — name, logo, description, colors fed at generation.
- **Catalog/operational data (b):** Mono pulls Google Business Profile data; GoDaddy Airo pulls business-listing data; e-commerce integrations (Ecwid on Yola/Brizy, BaseKit Store) accept product/catalog data. Vendasta and BaseKit accept services/listings via their broader SMB suites.
- **Behavioural/transactional data (c):** BaseKit markets "Growth insights" — acquisition, retention, custom-product-activation trend data — but this is analytics FROM the platform TO the partner, not customer transactional data feeding site generation. **No supplier was found to ingest end-customer transactional/behavioural data (bookings, purchases, no-shows, revenue) to DECIDE site content, emphasis, ordering, or structure.** A clear negative result across the set.
- **When used:** predominantly one-time at generation (Mono/Airo use GBP data at generation). Continuous, data-driven site restructuring based on transactional signals is claimed by nobody.
- **Populate vs. decide:** overwhelmingly populates fields; no evidence of data deciding structure.
- **DPA / subprocessor structure:** BaseKit is UK GDPR / Data Use and Access Act 2025 compliant with localized data-residency hosting; Mono emphasizes GDPR and EU data residency; both position the partner as controller and themselves as processor/subprocessor. Detailed DPA terms are not public — negative result.

### Post-creation lifecycle

- **Trigger for change:** predominantly human edit. Supplier-side template/platform updates propagate automatically in hosted models (Ucraft: "every element, integration, or major core update added to Ucraft will automatically appear on your platform"; BaseKit ships bi-weekly releases). Scheduled data-sync-driven or performance-signal-driven auto-changes: not found.
- **Editor model:** persistent canvas (drag-and-drop) is the norm — BaseKit, Site.pro, Duda, Simvoly, Ucraft, Weblium, Sitejet. Regenerate-on-demand (Durable, B12) is used by the direct-to-SMB AI builders. Hybrid (AI generate then persistent canvas edit): 10Web, GoDaddy Airo, Mono.
- **Conflict between regeneration and edits:** the persistent-canvas suppliers would overwrite user edits if a full regenerate were triggered; this is precisely why the deep-OEM suppliers do NOT auto-regenerate. No supplier claims automated post-creation outcome improvements from regeneration.

### Performance, SEO, accessibility

- **Independently measured:** Duda's Core Web Vitals leadership was established in prior work (HTTP Archive / CrUX). For other suppliers, no independent CrUX/Lighthouse data was surfaced — a gap. BaseKit (ImageEngine CO2/speed), Site.pro (Nginx speed), and GoHighLevel (managed CDN) claims are vendor-claimed, not independently verified here.
- **SEO structure:** most offer meta tags, sitemaps, header hierarchy, blog/CMS. uKit exposes alt/meta/H1–H4. GoHighLevel covers page-level SEO but "does not match the depth of a dedicated WordPress SEO plugin like Yoast/RankMath" per hands-on reviewers.
- **Accessibility:** BaseKit claims WCAG 2.1 AA across products. Independently verified: not found — negative result.

### Adjacent surfaces

- **Native mobile app layer:** none of the core suppliers offer a native mobile app-building layer as part of the website OEM; Universe is app-first but direct-to-user. A genuine gap.
- **E-commerce/bookings/CRM to same partners:** BaseKit (Store + Bookings + mini-CRM), Mono (Ecommerce+, Scheduling+ via Timify), Vendasta (full SMB suite), GoHighLevel/Simvoly/DashClicks (CRM, funnels, email, appointments), Durable/B12 (CRM, invoicing, scheduling). Bundling adjacent SMB tools is the dominant expansion play.

### Commercials

- **Published partner pricing (rare):** Site.pro — White Label $140/mo for 500 premium sites ("$0.28 for a website per month"), scaling down per site; revenue-share alternative (partner earns 50% / "100% earn per sale" depending on model). Site.pro also frames the market norm as "an average White Label website builder's plan costs $149/month" with "less than 10" sites and "$14/month" per extra site. SiteSwan — Starter $149/mo (10 sites), Agency $199/mo (20 sites), Pro $300/mo (100 sites); resellers keep 100% of retail. Simvoly — WL Basic $59/mo, WL Advanced $129/mo. Brizy — Agency $199/mo (API), Enterprise custom. IM Creator — "Host it yourself" $500/mo; "Servers control" $25k/yr; billing 5% fee. Ucraft — ~$599/mo Agency, ~$1,999/mo Partner (third-party sourced, verify). 10Web/Duda white-label tiers exist but OEM economics are quote-based.
- **Undisclosed (negative result):** BaseKit ("competitive RRP," bespoke), Mono (no public rate card), Yola (custom), Duda-OEM (custom/enterprise), WebPros/Sitejet (bundled into cPanel/Plesk license — effectively $0 marginal to the hosting partner). Reddit/Indie Hackers/hosting-forum searches did not surface leaked partner rate cards — genuine negative.
- **What the platform charges tenants (margin signal):** Simvoly-on-GoHighLevel agencies typically charge clients $97–$297/mo for white-label SaaS access; SiteSwan suggests resellers charge $500–$1,500 setup + $29–$99/mo. Against a ~$0.28–$10/site wholesale cost, this implies large gross margins — the category's core economic attraction.
- **Revenue share:** Site.pro and uCoz/uKit historically offer revenue-share (no setup fee) as an alternative to fixed licensing. Duda explicitly does NOT share revenue on partner widgets (prior work).

### Adoption and evidence

- **Named partners with verticals:** BaseKit → Telefónica, Etisalat, Fasthosts, Miss Group, Sri Lanka Telecom (telco/hosting); Mastercard (payments/inclusion). Mono → Orange (telco, France), PA Digital (Spain directories), Ecwid, Mastercard MBOT/BCSG. Duda → SaaS/hosting (prior work). 10Web → unnamed "top 10 hosting company." WebPros/Sitejet → cPanel/Plesk hosting ecosystem.
- **Scale claims with composition:** most claims (Duda ~1M sites/22k orgs; 10Web 2M generations/1,000 partners; Mono 250k subscriptions; Durable 6M+ then 10M+ sites; B12 1M+ businesses) lack published methodology or composition (created vs. live-and-paid; agency vs. platform vs. direct). BaseKit's most specific partner-level figures come from its Telefónica-Acens case study: "one of the world's leading telcos with 322+ million clients chose BaseKit... 42% reduction in customer churn," and "BaseKit powered 40,000 sites for Acens in six months" — both vendor-sourced.
- **Fraction of tenants who publish/maintain a site:** UNKNOWN across the entire set — not published by any supplier.
- **Stickiness once integrated:** BaseKit cites 62% of SMBs as unlikely to change provider (vendor-sourced).
- **Platform-side friction (partner perspective):** BaseKit markets ~6–8 week integration and 2,000 sites/month migration capacity; 10Web claims "under two weeks"; Duda case studies claim 5–6 weeks. Rate limits, provisioning constraints, deprecations, and forced migrations from the partner perspective were not found in public reviews — reviews overwhelmingly reflect the end-user perspective, a genuine sourcing gap.

## Category History and Segmentation

**Ten-year arc — consolidation under strategics and PE:**
- **2018:** Per Square's April 26, 2018 press release/SEC 8-K, "Square, Inc. (NYSE: SQ) has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Weebly. Square will pay a mix of cash and stock of approximately $365 million"; Weebly had "more than 625,000 paid subscribers," ~40% outside the US. Weebly's standalone brand was later de-emphasized inside Square/Block.
- **2019:** Bauer Media Group acquires Mono Solutions (July 18).
- **2019–2023:** Hostinger launches Zyro (2019), then rebrands it into Hostinger Website Builder (2023) and de-markets the Zyro brand.
- **2021:** WebPros acquires Sitejet, later shipping it natively into cPanel/Plesk. Duda raises its $50M Series D (last round to date).
- IONOS acquires WE22/CM4all (white-label builder, 5M+ sites).
- **2023–2024:** Bookmark shuts down after failing to raise; AiDA AI not acquired.
- **2023:** Durable ($14M Series A, Dec 13), GoDaddy Airo, IONOS AI generator (Sept 12), Jimdo Dolphin lineage — the AI-generation wave.
- **January 9, 2025:** Mono Solutions acquired by the FCR Media + 1881 Group + heise consortium.
- **August 19, 2025:** WebPros ships free AI Website Generator in Sitejet across cPanel/Plesk.

**Segmentation — the category splits by buyer:**
- **Hosting/registrar segment:** Site.pro, WebPros/Sitejet, 10Web, IONOS/CM4all, uKit/uCoz, BaseKit. Control-panel distribution dominates; pricing is per-site/volume or bundled into panel licenses.
- **Telco/bank segment:** BaseKit (Telefónica, Etisalat, Sri Lanka Telecom, Mastercard) and Mono (Orange) are the concrete named players; on-premise/data-residency and dedicated account management are differentiators.
- **Vertical/horizontal SaaS + agency segment:** Duda, 10Web, Vendasta, Brizy, Simvoly, GoHighLevel, DashClicks, SiteSwan, Weblium, Ucraft. API-embed and reseller dashboards dominate.

Different suppliers clearly dominate different segments: BaseKit and Mono own the telco lane; WebPros and Site.pro own the control-panel/hosting lane; Duda and 10Web straddle SaaS/hosting.

**Build vs. buy.** GoDaddy (Airo), Wix, Squarespace, Hostinger, IONOS, and GoHighLevel all built (or acquired and now own) their builders in-house rather than licensing an OEM supplier — because the builder is core and scale justifies the engineering. IONOS is a documented build-by-acquisition case (WE22/CM4all). The buy side is populated by platforms for whom websites are adjacent, not core: telcos (Telefónica→BaseKit, Orange→Mono), hosting resellers, and agencies. **No clean public case of a platform switching from buy to build (or vice versa) was found — a negative result.**

## What the Evidence Does and Doesn't Support

**Established fact:**
- A genuine embedded/white-label supply market exists and is 15+ years old, with concrete named telco/bank deployments (Telefónica, Etisalat, Sri Lanka Telecom via BaseKit; Orange via Mono).
- Control-panel distribution (cPanel/Plesk via WebPros/Sitejet and Site.pro) is a major channel; WebPros's free AI generator (Aug 19, 2025, with layout/structure claims) and Sitejet's native panel integration are documented.
- Ownership is consolidating (Mono, Sitejet, Weebly, Zyro, CM4all all under strategics/PE).
- Output portability is poor except for WordPress-based suppliers (10Web, Vendasta); SiteSwan and uKit are explicitly non-portable.
- Published partner pricing exists only for Site.pro, SiteSwan, Simvoly, Brizy, and (third-party) Ucraft/Weblium; it is bespoke/undisclosed for BaseKit, Mono, Duda-OEM, Yola.
- AI content-generation-into-templates is near-universal; data-fed generation (GBP data) shipped at Mono (Aug 1, 2024) and GoDaddy Airo.

**Open and unverified:**
- Whether any supplier does genuine layout/structure synthesis (10Web "agentic," Site.pro conversational editor, WebPros "layout, structure" claims) — no independent verification.
- Standalone revenue for most suppliers (Mono, BaseKit, Site.pro, 10Web); Mono headcount conflicts (30 vs 51–200).
- Mono's reported 2022 Ten Oaks Group ownership step (aggregator-sourced only).
- The fraction of a partner's tenants who actually publish/maintain a site — UNKNOWN for every supplier.
- Independent Core Web Vitals/Lighthouse data for suppliers other than Duda.
- Partner-side friction (rate limits, deprecations, forced migrations) — reviews reflect end-user, not partner, perspective.
- Detailed DPA/subprocessor terms — not public.
- Whether any supplier ingests end-customer transactional/behavioural data to decide site content/structure — found for nobody.

**Negative results:**
- No core website-builder OEM supplier identified in this research offers a native mobile-app-building layer embedded into partner platforms. Universe is app-first but sells direct-to-user rather than as an embedded supplier. The embedded-native-app supplier market — the app analog of this category — was not populated by any supplier found here; it is under-researched and warrants dedicated follow-up.

## Caveats
- Most scale figures (sites, organizations, partners, subscriptions) are VENDOR-CLAIMED with no published methodology, sample, or composition (created vs. live-and-paid; agency vs. platform vs. direct). Treat all such numbers as marketing until independently corroborated.
- Partner/OEM pricing is genuinely undisclosed for the major deep-OEM suppliers (BaseKit, Mono, Duda-OEM, Yola); the negative result is a finding, not a research shortfall.
- Several data points conflict and are shown, not averaged: BaseKit "60+" vs "100+" partners; Mono "50+" vs "70+" partners and headcount 30 vs 51–200; Mono's 2022 Ten Oaks step is aggregator-only.
- Independent performance (CrUX/Lighthouse) and accessibility (WCAG) verification exists in prior work only for Duda; for all others, conformance is vendor-claimed.
- AI capability and pricing move fast; re-verify any figure older than ~18 months. WebPros AI (Aug 2025), Mono Quick Creator AI (Aug 2024), IONOS AI (Sept 2023), and GoDaddy Airo (2025) are the freshest data points.
- This research was constrained on some non-US/APAC/LATAM and vertical-SaaS-specific suppliers by search budget. The "vertical-SaaS-embeds-a-builder" segment (e.g. field-service, restaurant, real-estate SaaS embedding a site layer) is likely under-sampled and merits dedicated follow-up, as does confirmation of Mono's 2022 ownership step and independent performance benchmarking of non-Duda suppliers.
