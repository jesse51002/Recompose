# Tier-Unlock ROI

The shape of the value argument: sell a platform a **revenue tier it cannot currently scale**, not end-user satisfaction. The constraint being removed is elapsed human time per tenant — 3–6 weeks wherever a turnaround is published — which caps how many branded apps a platform can sell regardless of demand. Nothing in the argument depends on tenants liking their app more.

Per-tenant theming is already sold as a priced tier: the embedded-analytics white-label ladder makes level 3 explicitly per-tenant theming for reseller scenarios. That matters because the vault's competitor research establishes that **end users do not pay for brand depth** — app-store reviews of Subsplash, Mindbody and Olo apps complain about bugs and slowness, never about looking generic. Pricing it as a tier makes that finding irrelevant rather than damaging.

**The deliberate prohibition:** the pitch is never *"your tenants will love it."* The two halves that are allowed are *tier-unlock* (a premium tier bounded by people rather than by demand) and *lost deals* (competitive deals lost to whoever's app can actually look like the prospect's brand) — the loss-framed half adapted from Gigacatalyst's own problem statement, which lands with a sales leader where the margin argument only lands with a CFO.

**It is a throughput argument, not a cost-savings argument, and the distinction is load-bearing.** The design functions inside these platforms are small and cheap: ~4 people at Circle who primarily do the company's own marketing design, a $499 flat fee at Vagaro, $99–169 one-time at ABC Trainerize, and professional services running 1.19% of revenue at Olo and 2.9% at Toast. There is no payroll large enough to build a savings pitch on. The premium prices in circulation are **tier prices** — covering migration, a named strategist, App Store operations and higher limits, set by the size of the tenant's business — not design prices. What is scarce is capacity.

**The sharpest wedge is platforms where every tenant already has their own app** — Subsplash, Passion.io, Q2, Banno, Alkami. On a platform that sells a shared shell with a branded app as an upgrade, only 0.1–3% of tenants take it, and that rate tracks tenant size rather than price, so the tenant count and the addressable app count differ by roughly a hundredfold. Because the product changes structure and not only assets, the budget being attacked is the **custom-build** budget ($10–50k, 4–12 weeks in the agency market), not the theming budget.

**Recompose's own pricing is undefined.** T7 and Open Question 2 of the office hours doc record it as entirely open for this ICP — per-tenant, per-seat, platform license, or revenue share on the tier are all unresolved, and Gigacatalyst publishes no pricing, so the nearest analog gives no comparable. The one figure ever written down is $100/month per branded tenant in `YC_Application_F26.md`, and that doc itself labels it as the platform's COGS to defend as a range, never as a final number. The old $100/$300/$500 gym-owner ladder does not transfer and is dead. This concept is the *shape* of the ROI argument, not a price.
