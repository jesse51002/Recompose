# Tier-Unlock ROI

The shape of the value argument: sell a platform a **revenue tier it cannot currently staff**, not end-user satisfaction. Nothing in the argument depends on tenants liking their app more.

**The constraint being removed is engineering capacity.** Thirteen of nineteen platforms refuse per-tenant feature requests outright; the six that accept them staff named engineering organisations — Banno's Custom Development team, Backbase's Services org, Tapcart's Solutions Engineer at $120–140k — and gate every price behind sales. Supply is bounded by engineers, who are the expensive constraint.

**Throughput is the safer argument even though a cost argument is now available**, because no platform publishes what a custom build costs it.

**The deliberate prohibition:** the pitch is never *"your tenants will love it."* App-store reviews of white-labeled apps complain about bugs and slowness, essentially never about the app itself — the pain of templating is the B2B buyer's, not the end user's. The two halves that are allowed are *tier-unlock* (a tier bounded by engineering capacity rather than by demand) and *lost deals* (deals lost to whoever can supply the workflow the prospect operates on), the second taken directly from Gigacatalyst's own problem statement, which lands with a sales leader where a margin argument only lands with a CFO.

**Do not build a cost-savings pitch on design.** The design functions inside these platforms are small and cheap: ~4 people at Circle who primarily do the company's own marketing design, a $499 flat fee at Vagaro, $99–169 one-time at ABC Trainerize, and professional services running 1.19% of revenue at Olo and 2.9% at Toast. There is not enough cost there to save. Premium prices in circulation are **tier prices** — covering migration, a named strategist, App Store operations and higher limits, set by the size of the tenant's business.

**The sharpest wedge is platforms that already fund per-tenant custom development** — Banno, Backbase, Tapcart, Trainerize Enterprise, GoodBarber, BuildFire — sorted within that by whether every tenant already has their own app. The budget being attacked is a **development** line item, not a theming one.

**Recompose's own pricing is undefined.** Per module, per tenant, platform licence, or a share of what the platform charges — all unresolved, and Gigacatalyst publishes no pricing, so the nearest analog gives no comparable. The $100/month per branded tenant in [[Strategy/YC_Application_F26|YC_Application_F26.md]] was priced against designs and does not transfer; the $100/$300/$500 gym-owner ladder is dead. **This concept is the shape of the ROI argument, not a price.**
