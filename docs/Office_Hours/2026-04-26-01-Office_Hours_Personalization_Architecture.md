## Glossary

Shorthand used throughout this doc. References the 4/25 session and the architecture options doc; reproduced here so this doc reads standalone.

**V1** — version 1 of the engineering build. What ships in roughly 3 weeks for gym 1's pilot. The "current product" the demo describes today.

**V2** — version 2 of the engineering build. What gets added after gym 1's pilot data lands, roughly 6-10 weeks from now. The "what's coming next" in the roadmap section of a demo.

**P1 through P7** — premises. A premise is a claim the strategy depends on. *Locked* premises are high-confidence beliefs ("we believe this is true and we're betting on it"); *to-test* premises are explicit hypotheses ("we don't yet know, gym 1 will tell us"). P1-P4 carry from the 4/25 session; P5-P7 are new in this session. Full text in the Premises section below.

**Option 1 through Option 7** — the seven personalization-architecture options enumerated in `Business/Office_Hours/2026-04-26-00-Personalization_Architecture_Options.md`. Quick reference:

- **Option 1**: per-student coach feedback (~5 min/class of coach time per class). Strongest personalization, highest coach friction.
- **Option 2**: algorithmic floor only — no coach action, recommendations driven by class type + rank + history.
- **Option 3**: per-instance 30-second class description by coach. No per-student feedback, just "here's what today's class covered."
- **Option 4**: per-student feedback for new members only (first ~4 weeks).
- **Option 5**: members tap which topics were covered after class; system aggregates.
- **Option 6**: member sets a focus goal at onboarding ("improve takedowns"); system weights content against the goal.
- **Option 7**: layered architecture, multiple cheap personalization layers (A, B, C) plus coach feedback (D) as an optional top layer.

	**Layers A through D** — substructure inside Option 7. Layer A = algorithmic floor; Layer B = member goals; Layer C = gym-owner curriculum (set before classes periodically); Layer D = coach voice notes / per-student feedback (the high-cost layer).

**Concierge MVP / Wizard-of-Oz** — a pre-engineering test where the founder hand-runs a feature with one real customer to measure actual behavior before committing code. Tony Xu drove DoorDash's deliveries personally for the first year using this pattern. The point isn't to scale; it's to find out what the actual job feels like in the hands of a real person before building software for it.

**Floor / floor product** — the always-on baseline personalization that doesn't require any per-class coach action. Features that work for every gym, every class, every member, automatically.

**Tier C demand evidence** — terminology from the 4/25 session. Tier A = paid customer behavior; Tier B = verbal "yes, I'll buy at this price"; Tier C = warm interview response, no commitment. CombatDen is currently at Tier C.

## What this session changed

### P4 is being stress-tested, not broken

Last session's P4 said: *"CombatDen handles everything else (engagement, progression, loyalty, coach feedback) with minimal coach workload."*

Option 1 from the architecture doc (per-student coach feedback, ~5 min/class) directly violates "minimal coach workload." The founder's stated preference is Option 1, but the founder also named coach adoption as the unproven assumption. P4 is therefore not broken — it is the explicit hypothesis being tested. If gym 1's coach completes the post-class loop reliably, P4 was true and Option 1 unlocks. If the coach skips, P4 was correct by design and the product stays in floor-only territory. Either result is good for the founder; it just gates V2 architecture on real behavior.

### Retention is the explicit job for personalization, not moat or differentiation

Founder explicitly rejected moat as a justification. ("I've never said anything about moat.") The job for the personalization layer is retention. That commits the analysis to evidence-of-retention math rather than long-term-defensibility math.

### Evidence-base re-weighting

- The 33% in-person staff-interaction stat halved (15%) lands inside the published gamification range (15-50%), so the two stats reinforce rather than stack. Loyalty has stronger published evidence than personalization, but the gap is smaller than it first appeared.
- The Value_Pricing doc's "61-74% retention in branded-app studies" claim is sourced to studies that mix gamification, branded-app effects, and personalization. V1 (without Option 1) delivers two of the three pillars (class-aware content, rank-tuned recommendations) but not the third (coach feedback). The 61-74% sales claim either needs to be qualified to what V1 actually delivers or sourced more precisely.

### Coach adoption is the load-bearing unknown, and it cannot be tested pre-pilot

The founder named "whether coaches will actually do it" as the weakest link in Option 1. There is no pre-product test for this. Interviews don't predict revealed behavior. Paper-prototype validation gives you stated intent, which is not the question. The only test that gives revealed-behavior signal is a Wizard-of-Oz / concierge-MVP loop run with a real coach at a paying gym for 14 days.

## The Converged Architecture (Sequencing, Not Choice)

The personalization architecture decision becomes a sequencing decision once you accept that Option 1 cannot be derisked before gym 1 closes.

### V1 (engineering committed now, ships in 3 weeks)

Six personalization vectors, zero coach action required:

1. **Algorithmic floor** — class type + member rank + class history + "haven't seen yet" filter
2. **Loyalty engine** — points from attendance, streaks, division progression, tier promotions; activity feed; gym-branded merch redemption
3. **Combat-sports structure** — Beginner 1/2/3 division ladder, tier promotions (Beginner → Novice → Intermediate → Advanced → Elite) with celebration moments
4. **Pre-class technique preview** — per-class-type video shown on booking confirmation
5. **Member goals at onboarding** — focus area set in week 1, refreshed at promotions / streaks / hiatuses (Option 6 from the architecture doc)
6. **Gym-owner curriculum at gym onboarding** — owner maps the schedule to topic blocks once, app uses it for per-week per-instance precision without coach daily action (Layer C from Option 7)

This is not Option 2. It is not Option 7-deferred-Option-1. It is a multi-layer floor where four of the six vectors are personalization-active without coach involvement.

### Concierge gate (gym 1 pilot, weeks 1-2)

Once gym 1 signs:

- Founder runs the rotation manually. Google Sheet or paper. No app changes.
- Coach records voice memo at end of class on their phone, sends to founder (text, AirDrop, whatever is frictionless).
- Founder transcribes and delivers to members via SMS or email that night, formatted manually.
- Two weeks. Measure: coach skip rate, average time per class, qualitative coach feedback at end of week 1 and week 2, member response rate.
- Cost: founder time during onboarding window. No engineering committed.

### V2 architecture decision (after concierge test, week 3 of pilot)

- If coach skip rate <20% and coach experience is positive: build Option 1 as V2. Add the surfacing logic, rotation queue, AI-formatting, per-student delivery to the engineering plan.
- If coach skip rate >50% or coach experience is negative: V1 floor stays as the product. Rewrite Pitch.md and Value_Pricing.md to reflect what V1 actually delivers. Combat-sports structure + loyalty + goals + curriculum becomes the long-term differentiation story rather than coach feedback.
- If skip rate falls between 20-50%: re-test at gym 2. Same concierge protocol, different coach. Two coaches' behavior is more signal than one.

## Open Conviction Question (founder reserved 24-48h)

The unresolved question from this session: *Is the V1 product (six vectors, no coach feedback) sellable with conviction by the founder?*

This is not a question about the product. It is a question about the founder's belief in the product. Until that belief is named honestly, every demo will leak doubt and every prospect will feel it.

The two paths that follow from the answer:

- **If yes:** ship V1 as described. Demo with conviction. Mention Option 1 as the V2 roadmap built with gym 1's coaches via the concierge test. Pitch stays loss-framed retention.
- **If no:** the architecture decision becomes the surface symptom of a deeper problem. The founder believes Option 1 is what makes the product real, and ships V1 anyway. That mismatch breaks the demo. The harder choice in that case is to delay launch by 4-6 weeks, build Option 1 in V1 engineering, and accept the coach-adoption risk in production. This trades speed for conviction.

The 4/25 session's recommended path (close gym 1 in 3 weeks) holds regardless. What's at stake in the 24-48h conviction call is what V1 actually contains when gym 1 demos and signs.

- **P4** — Coaches will reliably complete the post-class Option 1 loop. Test via concierge MVP at gym 1, weeks 1-2 of pilot.
- **V1 conviction (founder)** — Founder can demo V1 (six vectors, no coach feedback) with conviction. Resolved within 48h via the assignment below, before gym 1's closing call.
- **PushPress differentiation** — V1's combat-sports structure (ranks, divisions, gym-branded merch, member goals, owner curriculum) is enough differentiation in a 60-second demo. Validated in real demos starting with gym 1's closing call. Open question carried over from 4/25.

## Approaches Considered

The seven options in `Business/Office_Hours/2026-04-26-00-Personalization_Architecture_Options.md` were re-examined against the retention-job framing:

- **Option 1 alone** — rejected for V1 because coach adoption is unproven; remains the V2 candidate pending concierge results.
- **Option 2 alone** — rejected as too thin for V1 demos; the per-instance gap is real and members would notice.
- **Option 3 (per-instance coach description)** — rejected by the same coach-adoption logic as Option 1; if a coach won't do 5 min/class for per-student feedback, they likely won't do 30 sec/class for class summary either, and we have no way to test the difference pre-pilot.
- **Option 4 (new-member-only)** — rejected by the founder. Two-tier experience creates "unfair" perception (the founder's word), and the post-window degradation creates a downgrade moment for tenured members.
- **Option 5 (member tagging)** — set aside. Member friction in a member-facing app is a brand mismatch, and small class sizes (5-15) make aggregation unreliable.
- **Option 6 (member goals)** — promoted into the V1 floor. Cheap, no coach friction, real personalization vector.
- **Option 7 (layered)** — closest to the converged architecture, but the framing was rejected by the founder ("Option 1 with a skip step"). The substance survives without the label: V1 contains layers A, B, and C (algorithmic, goals, curriculum); Layer D (coach feedback) is gated on the concierge result.

**The loyalty headline question:** loyalty does not headline V1 because personalization was weakened. Personalization remains in the product story as the V2 promise built collaboratively with gym 1's coaches via concierge.

## The Assignment

Two things in the next 24-48 hours, before the gym 1 closing follow-up.

### 1. Resolve the V1 conviction question out loud

Pick a quiet hour. Pretend you're sitting across from the gym 1 owner. Pitch V1 as currently scoped: loss-framed retention headline, the six personalization vectors as the mechanism, combat-sports structure (ranks, divisions, branded merch) as the PushPress differentiator, Option 1 as a V2 roadmap item if they ask about coach feedback. Time it. Record yourself if you can.

Then play it back and ask: does this sound like the energy of *"software to stop members from quitting"* — the language you used naturally with Aztec and Global MMA — or does it go flat at the personalization section?

If it lands sharp, conviction is there. Ship V1 as scoped. The architecture is decided.

If it goes flat, you have your answer too. The honest move then is to delay the gym 1 close by 4-6 weeks, commit Option 1 to V1 engineering, accept the coach-adoption risk in production, and tell gym 1 that the closing call moves to mid-June. That's a real cost and you should make that trade consciously, not by drift.

The 4/25 assignment to follow up with the 2-day-old warm gym TODAY still holds. The conviction question is a 1-hour exercise. It does not block today's email.

### 2. Spec the concierge protocol now, before gym 1 signs

While you wait for gym 1 to commit, write the 1-page concierge protocol you'll run in weeks 1-2. Include:

- The rotation rule (which 5 students surfaced per class, refreshed how often)
- The coach's exact post-class action (record voice memo on phone, send to founder how)
- Your manual loop (transcription window, formatting template, delivery channel — SMS or email)
- The metrics you'll track (skip rate, average time, member response, coach qualitative weekly check-in)
- The decision rule (skip rate <20% → build V2; >50% → ship floor only; in-between → re-run at gym 2)

Spec'ing this before gym 1 signs means the moment they say yes, you can start week 1 of the pilot the next day. Not specced means you lose 3-5 days at the start of the pilot fumbling the manual loop, which directly compresses your 14-day window.
